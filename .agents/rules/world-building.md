## THE CROWN OF RATHERLON (Artifact)

Not a dainty fairy-tale tiara — an ancient, heavy, battle-weathered circlet of state.
- Originally forged under the old Rathen kings, later claimed by the Brennan dynasty (Aldren I through IX)
- Metal: Deep antique burnished gold and dark bronze, subtle battle patina, fine micro-scratches, age
- Engravings: Intricate archaic reliefs — subtle wyvern/bat-like wing arches, wheat grain bands (nodding to Brennan farm roots), ancient geometric reed patterns
- Gemstones: Sparse, dark, deep-cut (raw emerald, smoky quartz, or river-sapphire) in heavy bezel settings, not flashy prongs

## KINGDOM NAME & ETYMOLOGY

**Formal name (locked):** the **Kingdom of Ratherlon**. **Everyday speech:** *the realm* / *Ratherlon*.

People still say "the old crown" or "Rathen days" when they mean the centuries when House Rathen wore the throne. The land's name did not change when the dynasty did.

**Ratherlon → House Rathen (linguistic drift):**
1. Early settlers of the central plains: scouts said the crossing was "a rather long piece of land." The phrase hardened into slang: *the Rather-Long*. Scribes smoothed it into **Ratherlon**.
2. Early border-lords of the crossroads were "X of the Rather-Long" → "the Rather-Long lord."
3. The ruling line shed *-lon* and became the **Rathen** line (spoken shortening). The family accepted the name the continent already used.
4. Court polish: heralds locked the spelling **House Rathen** and invented dignified glosses. The muddy walk stayed buried.

**Today:** Land = *Ratherlon*. Old dynasty = *House Rathen*. New dynasty = *House Brennan*, still kings *of Ratherlon* (Aldren I kept the kingdom's name for legitimacy).

## CLIMATE

**Southern hemisphere** — winter and ice come from the **south**; warmth, wine, and soft living from the **north**.

Ratherlon sits in the **middle of a wide continent** — very elongated east-west, the longest landmass on its axis. The kingdom of Ratherlon is the **longest kingdom** on this continent, bordered by other kingdoms (including the **Ashen Kingdom** / *the Ashlands* to the east, and unnamed **Western Kingdoms** to the west). Author mnemonic only: "rather long" crossing. Do NOT lecture that pun in prose.

## PRE-RATHEN HISTORY & RATHEN ORIGIN

**House Rathen's original name is lost.** They sailed from a distant land in the far northwest of the known world (beyond Vaelcross, not yet on the map). They landed in the Merelands — lakes, black soil, and no unified state to stop them.

**Why they expanded east, not west:**
- West: Vaelcross — a functioning kingdom with army, king, fortified settlements, established borders. Attacking it was unrealistic.
- East/inland: politically fragmented. Villages, farming communities, local headmen, regional councils — no unified state capable of resisting incorporation.

**The expansion was gradual, entirely voluntary, and never a conquest plan.** Over generations:
- Communities joined for protection, law, trade, and political organization
- Headmen swore allegiance because unity made life better, not because swords compelled them
- No one was forced, defeated, or coerced into joining — the Rathens did not fight their way east
- The Rathens simply offered what fragmented communities could not provide alone, and people chose it

**Expansion order (over generations):** Merelands → Western Shore → Deepwood → Vine Shore → Southfrost → Rivermark → Eastverge. Seven regions under the Rathen crown.

**The accidental superpower:** The Rathens didn't set out to build an empire. They entered the political vacuum because it was available. But by the time they finished, they had accidentally created the largest centralized state on the continent.

**Before Ratherlon:** Ashen Kingdom | scattered communities | Vaelcross Kingdom
**After Ratherlon:** Ashen Kingdom | massive unified kingdom | Vaelcross Kingdom

This changed the balance of power overnight. Neither neighbor had cared about the fragmented interior. Now both had an enormous foreign kingdom on their border. They didn't necessarily want to conquer Ratherlon — they wanted to contain it: border territory, river control, port access, trade privileges, buffer zones, tribute, preventing Ratherlon from becoming dominant. Centuries of border friction without requiring either neighbor to attempt full conquest.

*"We should have dealt with this before it became a kingdom."* — the essential Ashen and Vaelcross calculation, arrived at too late.

**The 8th region — the Crownfold:** When the Great Civil War broke out (~Y200) and Aldren I (Torrhen Brennan) ended it, he was crowned king. The Rathens had ruled 7 regions. Aldren I carved an 8th — the Crownfold — by taking a strip of land from every surrounding region, creating a neutral royal district at the heart of the kingdom. The Brennan family's Foldstead (farm + Nyxen roosts) was already there. The farmer's fields became the seat of the new dynasty.

## REGION BLURBS

**The Crownfold (Brennan):** The 8th region, carved by Aldren I from strips of every surrounding region when he ended the Great Civil War. Where the farmer's fields became a kingdom. Foldhall sits on a low rise above the confluence of two rivers. Not the richest land — the *legitimacy* land. Nyxen roosts carved into cliff-barns east of the city: half fortress, half sty, smelling of oil, straw, and ozone.

**The Merelands (Rathen):** Where the Rathens first landed, sailing from a distant land in the far northwest. Former royal house. Lakes and black soil. Seat Stillford. The first Rathen region and the base from which they expanded east. After the Great Civil War they kept Stillford as paramounts under Brennan. They sit on the Western Front, looking directly across at the Vaelcross Kingdom, dealing with forest skirmishes, mountain raids, and constant border tension. Queens into the Aldren line (Maris, Catelyn, Elyn) are old royal blood marrying the Nyxen dynasty.

**The Vine Shore (Varen):** Warm north. Harvest festivals, green glass, ships heavy with wine. Roselyn is this world's idea of spring. Killing her is killing the soft future Edric promised.

**The Southfrost (Norvalen):** Winter rides up from here. Fur in months the Vine Shore wears linen. Iron and timber. Tucked safely away at the bottom of the map, guarded by a massive wall of mountains. They are physically removed from the Ashen and Vaelcross threats and have no appetite for a devastating, aggressive war.

**The Western Shore (Tarnell):** Salt, rope, and ship-kings. They care about trade lanes more than which twin quotes better law. Dangerous neutrals.

**The Eastverge (Kelward):** Dust, watchtowers, and raid seasons. Harwin's kind of country. Seat: Eastgirt. The eastern scrublands are home to the **bront** — a massive single-horned beast, taller than any horse, endemic to the Eastverge grasslands. Kelward broke bronts to the saddle generations ago, and their border cavalry rides them instead of horses. A charging bront-line is a living shield wall; nothing stops them short of massed pikes or a Nyxen from the air. Other regions ride horses — bronts are a Kelward thing, and Kelward lords bring them when called to Foldhall, which is how the rest of the kingdom knows the animals.

**The Rivermark (Dorsen):** Whoever feeds the armies wins the long war. Both twins will send envoys here first. It sits on the Eastern Front, buffering the Ashen Kingdom. With a major river system snaking right out of the Ashen mountains through Rivermark, it is a prime highway for enemy naval or infantry invasions.

**The Deepwood (Selborn):** South of the grain, north of the true ice. Forests that remember when Nyxens were hunted. Selborn men post warning stones at the tree-line and do not thank city folk who ask for guides.

Same containment logic as Ashen — once the scattered interior became Ratherlon, the strategic calculation changed. The Rathens could not attack Vaelcross; now Vaelcross watches the kingdom that grew between it and Ashen.

## ALDREN THE ADVENTUROUS & THE BRENNAN CINDER

### Queen Maris & Leofric's Pre-Cinder Beauty

**Queen Maris Rathen** (Aldren II's queen) was the great beauty of her age — famed across the realm for her radiant skin and captivating presence. Aldren II was a fair and handsome king, but Maris was the true wonder. Leofric inherited her beauty in full. Histories record him as arguably the most beautiful prince who ever walked the realm — with flawless skin and striking features, already considered a perfect human specimen before any magic touched him. Every privilege, every luxury, every gift of blood and bone. And still he wanted only the wind in his face.

### The Story

**Aldren III (Leofric), called "the Adventurous."** Third king of the Brennan dynasty. Young, restless, heir to a kingdom his grandfather won and his father secured. The realm was at peace. Leofric wanted to see what lay beyond it.

He was told no. He was the heir. The dynasty had only three generations of legitimacy; a prince vanishing across the sea would crack what Aldren I bled to build.

Leofric proposed a bargain: if he married and produced an heir first, could he go? The king and queen agreed. A match was arranged with **Elen of House Varen** — Vine Shore blood, good alliance, a daughter of Rosegate.

**Leofric held up his end.** He married Elen. He consummated. He stayed long enough for her to quicken. Then he fled — north across the warm sea, into lands Ratherlon had only heard of through traders. Just he and his Nyxen, leaving behind a pregnant wife and a court that had believed the bargain meant he would stay.

**Leofric's Nyxen was an uncommon beauty** — where most Nyxens wore tawny-gold or wheat-tan fur, his bore a rare coat of radiant ash-charcoal with a gunmetal sheen, dark as storm-cloud but luminous in any light. An uncommon colouring that made the beast unmistakable among the roosts of Foldhall.

**Elen bore a son at Foldhall: Aldric Brennan.** Leofric's firstborn. Full Brennan scent. Full legal claim to the crown. The boy was born and raised at the royal seat under the protection of Aldren II and Queen Maris — not at Rosegate. He was the crown's child, acknowledged by the court.

### The Voyage

Leofric flew and sailed for months. He crossed the warm sea and reached the continent beyond — lands far from Ratherlon's knowledge. He discovered **Aurouvah**, a wealthy kingdom of ancient magic, great beauty, and permanent cosmetic arts unlike anything in Ratherlon. The people of Aurouvah practiced magical procedures that altered the body permanently: eye colour, skin, hair, bone structure. Beauty in Aurouvah was an art form backed by sorcery. Deeper rituals could reach into blood itself.

Leofric went incognito. He explored. He was already a handsome man — rich, well-bred, a runaway prince with a Nyxen — but Aurouvah showed him things he had never seen in a human before.

### The Brennan Cinder (cosmetic — NOT the curse)

Leofric underwent one of their cosmetic procedures. When the Aurouvian sorcerers asked what he wanted, he did not hesitate — he wanted to look like the creature that had carried him across the world. His Nyxen had been his only companion for months, the only piece of home he had left, and he wanted to wear its colours. The sorcerers permanently changed his hair to a deep, radiant **ash-charcoal** with a gunmetal sheen — not black, not grey, but a luminous dark that caught light like polished steel. The colour of his beast's rare coat, now his own. A rider's tribute, made permanent by foreign magic.

**This was harmless magic.** A vain, beautiful, loving act. The Cinder is NOT the curse. It is a separate magical event with a separate moral weight.

### Seraphine

In Aurouvah he met a woman: **Seraphine.** Noble-born, ash-dark hair of her own (natural in Aurouvah, where the colouring was common among the highborn), and eyes of deep teal — the same blue-green that lived in the Brennan sigil. He fell in love instantly.

They married. Seraphine conceived. **Her first child died during birth** — a stillbirth or dead within moments.

**The Aurouvian belief of rebirth:** The people of Aurouvah hold that a child who dies at birth passes its soul into the next child born to the same mother. This is not called superstition — it is a deeply held belief of a sophisticated magical culture, never proven false. Seraphine believed it. Her people believed it. Leofric, who had come to love Aurouvah and its ways, adopted the belief.

Seraphine conceived again and bore a living son. In Aurouvian tradition, this boy IS the first child — reincarnated, continuous, unbroken.

**But Leofric had doubts.** He knew the Nyxen scent runs through the body, not the soul. He knew that carrying a child nearly to term and losing it changes the mother's blood (the same way later pregnancies always produce weaker scent — the mother's body has been exposed). This living son might register to the Nyxens as a second child, not a first — weaker scent — regardless of what the Aurouvian beliefs said about his soul.

And even if the boy's scent was strong enough — there was still **Aldric** at Foldhall. A living son with undeniable legal claim and undeniable potency. Two heirs. One crown.

### The Blood Ritual (the curse)

Leofric could not accept it. He went to the Aurouvian sorcerers and asked for something far beyond cosmetic arts. He asked them to **transfer the heir's Nyxen scent from Aldric to Seraphine's living son.** To reach across the warm sea into his firstborn's blood and tear out the thing the Nyxens recognise — and give it to his love child instead.

**The sorcerers warned him.** They were forcing foreign magic onto a natural system they did not fully understand — the scent, the maternal inheritance, the way the blood governed itself. Layering sorcery onto something that had worked on its own for two hundred years would break something that could not be unbroken. Leofric paid them anyway.

**The ritual worked. The extraction killed Aldric.** At Foldhall, a healthy child sickened and died of "fever" before his second nameday. No one connected it to anything. Across the sea, Seraphine's son now carried the full scent.

**Magic is never clean.**

### Leofric Returns

Leofric sailed home with Seraphine and their son, back to a kingdom that had spent years grieving a vanished heir and now mourning a dead grandson. **When he returned, the realm barely recognised him.** Many swore it was an impostor — that the true prince had died in those distant northern lands. Leofric climbed down from his Nyxen looking like a living god. The Cinder magic had not merely changed his hair — it had seized upon the flawless beauty he already possessed, sharpened it, and burned it deeper into his flesh. The most beautiful prince in the realm had left; something almost unhuman had come back.

The Nyxens accepted Seraphine's boy without hesitation. The court accepted what the beasts accepted — because in Ratherlon, the beasts had always been the final word.

Leofric was crowned. Seraphine became queen. The Cinder — his sentimental rider's tribute — and Seraphine's Aurouvian blood combined with the Brennan bloodline to produce the hereditary ash-charcoal hair that would mark every Aldren from III onward. **The magic did not stop at hair.** It enhanced the existing Brennan beauty and made that enhancement hereditary — every generation after carried not just the metallic ash-charcoal hair but an almost otherworldly physical beauty that set them visibly apart from every other house in the realm. This is why the Brennans look "almost unhuman" — too fair, too striking, like statues of ancient legends rather than men of common blood. The farmer's blood is real. The beauty was made permanent by foreign magic that rewrote what "Brennan" looked like forever.

### The Brennan Cinder (inheritance)

**Inheritance rules (same pattern as the Nyxen riding trait):**
- Strongest in the firstborn heir of the direct line — full, vivid ash-charcoal with the metallic gunmetal sheen, near-identical to their father's and his father's before him. The firstborn trait is so dominant it overrides the maternal contribution almost entirely
- In second-born, third-born, and further siblings the Cinder blends with the mother's hair genetics — producing beautiful mixed tones that still carry a metallic radiance (dark brown shot through with steel, warm chestnut with a gunmetal sheen, etc.) but not the pure ash-charcoal. The maternal colour shows through more with each step from firstborn
- Fades further in cadet branches and distant cousins, until only a faint metallic lustre marks them as Brennan blood at all
- **Seraphine's teal eyes** appear irregularly — not every heir, but often enough to be remarked upon when they surface

**In-world, people remember Aldren the Adventurous as a romantic figure** — the prince who flew across the sea and brought home a beautiful foreign queen. The ash-charcoal hair is a point of pride: "He loved his beast so much he wore its colours." No one in Ratherlon knows the truth. Only the readers know the cost.

### The Varen Wound

Not just a jilted bride — a dead grandson. **Aldric Brennan** was born at Foldhall, died at Foldhall of "fever." The Varens can't prove anything, but they watched their daughter's child — a healthy boy with strong Brennan scent — sicken and die, and then a foreign queen's son sailed in and was accepted by every Nyxen in the roost. Two hundred years of quiet suspicion.

The betrothal of Roselyn Varen to Prince Edric ~200 years later is partly healing that ancient wound: Brennan blood returning to the line it was stolen from. When Harwin kills Roselyn, he rips open something far older than a broken engagement.

### The Curse (deep lore — readers know, characters do not)

The blood ritual broke the natural order. The maternal inheritance — the self-regulating system that had governed the Brennan line since the Farmer — was forced by foreign sorcery to skip a generation, to pretend one child was first when another was. The blood could not reconcile this. Every generation after, the inheritance fought itself: the natural order trying to reassert versus the magical override trying to hold.

**Every succession crisis after Aldren III is this contradiction manifesting.** The bloodline fights over who leads because the mechanism that used to decide it cleanly was broken:
- Queen Seren's war (Book 2): first female heir vs younger brother — the blood cannot settle on gender because it cannot settle on anything
- The Twin War (Book 1): two equal heirs, the realm splits — the curse at its worst, twin firstborns where the broken blood literally cannot choose
- The Returned Heir (Book 5): a descendant who refuses the crown and flees the continent — echoing Leofric's own flight, the restlessness repeating

The pattern: Leofric fled his pregnant wife and killed his own son with sorcery. Aldren X's great-grandson will flee his crown. The adventurous ancestor's crime echoes down the centuries because the magic demands it.

**This is NOT known within the realm.** No character will say "Aldren the Adventurous cursed us." The lore parallel is the Doom following the Targaryens — succession crisis after crisis, early deaths, bloodline self-destruction, no cadet branches that survive long. We the readers see the pattern. The characters see only their own war.

### The Aurouvian Reincarnation Belief

Stays **ambiguous forever.** Never confirmed, never denied. The reader holds both frameworks — the natural (scent, maternal inheritance) and the spiritual (soul-passage) — and decides for themselves whether Seraphine's living son truly was the dead firstborn reborn. Leofric chose to believe it. The sorcerers neither confirmed nor denied. The Nyxens can't tell a soul from a scent.

### The Aurouvian Language in House Brennan

Seraphine brought her mother tongue to Foldhall, and it never left. The Brennan royal family speaks **Aurouvian** as a private language — passed down through generations not as statecraft or ceremony, but as family intimacy. Unlike the Targaryens with High Valyrian, the Brennans do not use Aurouvian to command their Nyxens or impress foreign courts. They use it among themselves: in the nursery, in whispered asides, in muttered profanity after council meetings. A brother swearing under his breath in Aurouvian while his sibling snorts because he understood every word.

Leofric himself was terrible at the language — he mangled pronunciation and Seraphine laughed at him for it. The warmth of that comedy survived in the family long after both were gone.

### THE KINGSGUARD

Seven sworn swords who guard the king's person. Green cloaks (Brennan green) with a gold crown clasp at the throat. Led by the **Lord Commander of the Kingsguard.** Sworn for life; may not hold lands or titles while serving. Unlike ASOIAF's Kingsguard, they MAY marry — they are household knights, not monk-soldiers. When the king flies, at least two Kingsguard remain at the roost base; when on progress, all seven ride.

The Kingsguard guards the king, not the family. For the royal family's safety, the king assigns individual Kingsguard to attend specific members as needed.

### THE HEIR'S OWN

Three sworn swords assigned to the named heir. Green cloaks with a **silver** crown clasp (not gold — denotes service to the heir, not the king). Appointed by the king when an heir is formally named. When no heir is named, the Heir's Own stands empty — which is its own political statement. **At story open, neither twin has been given an Heir's Own, because Rodrik never named an heir.** The empty silver cloaks are a visible symbol of the succession crisis.

- **Green**: The Brennans descend from Farmer Brennan, a man of the soil. Green for agriculture, for the fields of the Foldstead, for the roots the dynasty never forgot. A farmer's colour on a king's banner.

## THE CALL-PIPE (Expanded Lore)

### Inventor & Origin

**Aldren III (the Adventurous) brought the call-pipe concept from Aurouvah.** During his years abroad, Leofric saw Aurouvian beast-keepers summon specific creatures from miles away using tuned mouth instruments whose tones encoded identity. He brought the idea home alongside Seraphine and the Cinder. Upon his restoration as king, he commissioned the first call-pipes for the Brennan riders.

**The Maester of Foldhall engineered the craft.** Maesters are an ancient institution in Ratherlon, predating any Brennan king (like the Citadel predates Aegon). What Aldren III imported was the concept; the Maester solved the acoustics — boring the interior, cutting protruding notches whose count shapes the harmonic. Only the Maester of Foldhall holds the bore geometry and notching technique. The knowledge passes by oral apprenticeship, Maester to Maester. No written blueprint exists.

### The Rider Roll

The roll begins with **Aldren III as Ordinal 1**. Riders before him — Aldren I, Aldren II, and their kin — are **"riders of the old way,"** uncounted and un-piped. They called their Nyxens by voice, by habit, by the bond itself. The numbered pipe is a royal institution, not a natural law.

**Roll-keeper:** The Maester of Foldhall. The rider roll is a physical document — vellum, the Maester's hand — kept alongside the pipe collection in the Foldhall armoury.

### The Fairest Pipes

Certain notch-counts produce a tone so pure that even the human ear hears the difference. This is acoustics, not magic: certain counts align the bore harmonics into something unmistakably beautiful, the way certain string lengths produce consonant intervals. Multiple counts across the range are "fair," but they are rare — most pipes sound alike to everyone but the Nyxen.

**The most celebrated sweet pipe** in Foldhall's memory belongs to an otherwise unremarkable rider — not a king, not a war hero, not a firstborn. The pipe outlived the rider's name in common memory; people remember the tone, not the person. The most beautiful sound in the dynasty belongs to someone history barely remembers.

**Specific ordinal number and rider name: TBD** when the full ordinal map is built (blocked until twin ordinals are fixed).

**Twin War political cue:** When the twins receive their ordinals, if one twin's count falls on a fair number, partisans will seize the coincidence as proof of divine favor — "even his pipe sings true." The Sunfyre principle: the world deals coincidences; people weaponize them into legitimacy. **Which twin: TBD with ordinal map.**

### Remake, Theft & Death Rules

- **Rider dies:** The pipe is kept in the Foldhall armoury as a relic. Never destroyed, never reassigned. The ordinal is retired permanently.
- **Pipe lost:** A replacement is made by the Maester of Foldhall — same ordinal, same notch-count, same tone. But this barely ever happens; pipes are kept close and rarely leave a rider's person.
- **Pipe stolen:** Exceedingly rare. A Nyxen knows where its rider is and can distinguish its rider's breath and position from a stranger sounding the same pipe miles away. A thief with a stolen pipe summons nothing but attention.

## DICTIONARY OF THE REALM (A-Z)

**Aldren.** Regnal name taken by every crowned Brennan king.
**Aldren the Adventurous.** Aldren III (birth name Leofric). Consummated with Elen of Varen, fathered Aldric at Foldhall, then fled across the warm sea to Aurouvah. Received the Cinder (cosmetic), married Seraphine, commissioned the blood ritual that killed Aldric and transferred the heir-scent. Returned with Seraphine and their son. Romantic figure in public memory; curse-bearer in deep lore.
**Aldric Brennan.** Leofric's firstborn son by Elen of Varen, born at Foldhall. Killed by the blood ritual before his second nameday — his Nyxen scent was extracted and transferred to Seraphine's son. The court recorded "childhood fever." The Varens remember.
**appanage.** Land/rents granted by king to younger kin.
**Aurouvah.** Wealthy kingdom on the far continent across the warm sea. A culture of ancient magic ranging from permanent cosmetic arts to deeper rituals that reach into blood. Where Leofric received the Cinder, married Seraphine, and commissioned the blood ritual. The Aurouvian belief of rebirth (a child who dies at birth passes its soul to the next) is deeply held and never disproven.
**Aurouvian.** Of or from Aurouvah. Also: the private language of House Brennan, inherited from Queen Seraphine — used among family for intimacy, profanity, and whispered asides, not ceremony or statecraft.
**banner.** The cloth device of a house. To "raise a banner" = declare for a cause.
**bannerman.** Lesser lord sworn to a greater one.
**birth name.** Name before crowning (Rodrik for Aldren IX).
**blood ritual.** The Aurouvian sorcery Leofric commissioned to transfer the Nyxen heir-scent from Aldric (his firstborn by Elen) to Seraphine's son. The extraction killed Aldric. The sorcerers warned that forcing foreign magic onto a natural system they did not understand would break something that could not be unbroken. They were right. NOT the same act as the Cinder — the Cinder was harmless cosmetic; the blood ritual was the curse.
**the Brennan Cinder.** The radiant ash-charcoal hair carried by the Brennan royal line since Aldren III married Seraphine of Aurouvah. Not black, not grey — a deep, luminous dark with a gunmetal sheen, as if each strand held a cold fire. Chosen by Leofric to match his Nyxen's rare ash-charcoal coat; made permanent by Aurouvian sorcerers as a harmless cosmetic rider's tribute. Combined with Seraphine's blood to become hereditary. Strongest in firstborn heirs; fades in younger siblings and distant branches. A point of dynastic pride in-world. The Cinder is NOT the curse — it is a separate magical act with a separate moral weight.
**bront.** Massive single-horned beast endemic to the Eastverge. Elasmotherium-inspired: stands half again as tall as a horse at the shoulder, dense russet-brown hide, thick seasonal coat, single forward-sweeping horn of bone-dense keratin longer than a man's arm. Broad flat skull, small eyes, enormously powerful neck and shoulders. Kelward broke them to the saddle generations ago and ride them instead of horses for border warfare. Named for the sound of their charge — a deep rolling thunder across the scrub. Stubborn, ill-tempered; a kick can kill a horse. But a broken bront is tireless, fearless, and carries an armored rider through terrain that would snap a horse's legs. Wild herds graze beyond the watch-stones. NOT a horse replacement kingdom-wide — bronts are a Kelward animal. A bront in the Vine Shore would be like an elephant in Venice.
**Crownfold.** Countryside around Foldhall.
**demesne.** Land held directly by a lord.
**smallfolk.** The common people of Ratherlon: farmers, tradesmen, fishermen, innkeepers — anyone without noble blood or title. Also called commonfolk. In prose, use "commoner" for a single person. Do NOT use "freeman" (echoes the wildlings' "free folk" from ASOIAF).
**frugivore.** Animal that lives chiefly on fruit. Nyxens are frugivores and herbivores.
**good-brother/sister/father/mother.** In-law terms, not praise.
**great house.** Paramount house ruling a wide region under the king.
**holdfast.** Small fortified dwelling; broader than "castle."
**litter.** A group of Nyxen young born together.
**ledger.** Book of accounts. Prince Rowan lives in them.
**Maester.** Learned men of Ratherlon — an ancient institution predating the Brennan dynasty. Healers, record-keepers, letter-writers, counselors. Every great house and many lesser lords keep a Maester. The Maester of Foldhall holds unique responsibilities: crafting call-pipes, maintaining the rider roll, and tending the pipe collection in the armoury.
**Nyxen.** Great flying beast; docile, frugivore, keen ears/nose, recognizes rare scent in farmer's bloodline.
**Nyxen oil.** Grooming substance for Nyxen fur. Smell clings to Foldhall's stones.
**paramount.** Highest under the king in a region.
**pipe / call-pipe.** Mouth instrument for a Nyxen rider. Custom begins under Aldren III (the Adventurous), who brought the concept from Aurouvah. Rider's ordinal cut as protruding notches inside; those notches shape the tone. Beasts know their own across miles. Some notch-counts praised as the sweetest pipes of the blood. Crafted only by the Maester of Foldhall. Do not use "whistle." See ## THE CALL-PIPE for full lore.
**progress.** Royal journey through the realm.
**pup.** Young Nyxen, about Golden Retriever puppy size (bigger counting wings). Must learn to lighten before true flight.
**reeve.** Village-level official who manages day-to-day running of a manor's farmland on behalf of the lord. Tracks planting, debts, small disputes. Not noble — a trusted commoner with authority over local order.
**realm.** Everyday speech for the Kingdom of Ratherlon.
**regnal name.** Name used while reigning. Always Aldren for Brennan kings.
**rider.** One who has raised/been accepted by a Nyxen. Each match written on the rider roll and given a call-pipe. Riders before Aldren III are "riders of the old way" — uncounted, un-piped.
**rider roll.** Running count of Brennan Nyxen riders since Aldren III. Kept by the Maester of Foldhall. Physical document — vellum, the Maester's hand — stored with the pipe collection in the Foldhall armoury.
**roar.** The Nyxen's directed pressure-blast. Not flame. Can stagger/kill at close range. Two beasts trading full roars can cripple each other.
**roost.** Where Nyxens are kept (at Foldhall, cliff-barns east of keep).
**roost-cold.** Chill left on a bed when someone rose early for the roosts. Not the deeper cold of a spouse at war. Household slang.
**sept-yard.** Open yard beside Foldhall's place of worship. People cut across on errands.
**shutters.** Wooden panels over windows.
**shout upwind.** Call so wind carries voice toward someone. Half-joke for "go find the king at the roosts."
**solar.** Private upper chamber; queen's work: petitions, letters, ribbons, quills.
**steward.** Officer who runs household stores, staff, daily order.
**wall-walk.** Path along top of castle wall.
**yard.** Open court inside castle walls.
