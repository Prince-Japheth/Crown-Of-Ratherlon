"You are not to ask me but choose the best, make the decision and make the edit wherever relevant while maintaining continuity."

**Why:** User wants creative momentum, not question-and-answer slowdown. They delegate judgment calls and expect edits to be made directly.

**How to apply:** When facing ambiguity in the book's world-building, narrative, or design — make the best decision and execute it. Explain the reasoning after, don't ask permission before.

**Writing style:** ASOIAF. Same institutional vocabulary unless explicitly changed below.

**POV structure (ASOIAF Multi-POV Third Person Limited):**
- One POV per chapter — camera locked to that character's perception for the entire chapter
- Strict limitations: only see what the POV character sees, only know what they know/think
- Unreliable narrators: characters may misremember history or misread intentions
- No omniscient narrator: if the POV character doesn't know a secret, the reader doesn't either
- **Chapter titles — Book 1 (ASOIAF Books 1-3 convention):** titled with the POV character's name in caps (RODRIK, ELYN, EDRIC, HARWIN, ROSELYN). Prologue/Epilogue stay generic. Table of contents adds Roman numerals for disambiguation (RODRIK I, RODRIK II).
- **Chapter titles — later books (ASOIAF Books 4-5 convention):** when a character undergoes an identity crisis, loses their name, or assumes a new persona, the chapter title shifts to a thematic/descriptive name (e.g., "The Survivor," "The Hand," "The Rider"). The name is literally taken off the chapter when the character loses their sense of self.
- **No POV blending within a chapter.** If it opens in Elyn's head, it stays in Elyn's head. Never jump to another character's internal perspective mid-chapter.

**ASOIAF terms we KEEP:** Maester, King's Guard, Hand of the King, Master of Whisperers (exists as a title — see plot note below), and all other standard court/feudal titles.

**ASOIAF terms we CHANGED:**
- Duke (not Lord Paramount) for great house heads
- Baron (not Lesser Lord) for lesser lords
- Handler (not Dragonkeeper) for Nyxen caretakers

**Master of Whisperers — plot note for later chapters:** The title EXISTS in Ratherlon. **Edric** (the diplomatic twin) will raise the argument that advertising spy operations by giving someone a public title meaning "The Person Who Collects Secrets" is a diplomatic disaster — it warns every visiting ambassador, lord, and lady exactly who to avoid at court. This becomes a character-revealing moment: Edric thinks about how power *looks*, not just how it works. Harwin (the warrior twin) wouldn't care about the optics.

**Verbal tic rules (thinned in prior sessions):**
- No repeating a character tag more than once in introduction. "Charcoal on his thumb" appears ONCE for Rowan, never again. Same for any visual motif.
- No metaphorical "ink" for writing/diplomacy/letters — just say "a letter," "write," "words." Physical ink (stains, blotted) is fine.
- No metaphorical "wings" for Nyxen power/presence/air support — just say what's happening. Physical wings (half-spread, folded) are fine.

- **No em dashes (—).** Use commas, semicolons, periods, or colons instead. ASOIAF barely uses them. The only acceptable use is a true mid-sentence interruption where a character is cut off mid-word.

**ABSOLUTE PROSE BANS (user flagged these across the entire book):**
- **NO PERSONIFICATION.** Never give inanimate objects or abstract concepts human agency. "The air asked it to be brave," "the silence judged him," "the stone remembered" — ALL BANNED. If the subject is not a person or animal, it does not think, feel, ask, judge, remember, forgive, or decide.
- **NO obscure or clever metaphors.** If a reader has to stop and decode the metaphor, it is too clever. "I hunt a dye, not a region" — BANNED. All metaphors must land INSTANTLY using feudal, seasonal, or blade imagery that any ASOIAF reader parses without effort. GRRM-style examples: "I seek a single neck for the headsman's axe," "My quarrel is with the wolf who bit, not the forest he runs in," "Words are wind," "A king who must say 'I am the king' is no true king." If it needs Googling, rewrite it.
- These problems exist throughout the current draft — a full prose audit is required.

**Writing complaints to avoid:**
- NO terse clipped dialogue like '"Before supper," Elyn said. "As promised."' or '"Names," Harrun said. "Not songs."' — user hates this pattern; it sounds nothing like ASOIAF
- Dialogue attribution must be CLEAR — never leave a quote unattributed when multiple speakers are present

**The Aurouvian language in House Brennan:**
- Seraphine brought her mother tongue to Foldhall; the royal family still speaks Aurouvian as a private language
- Used for intimacy, whispered asides, muttered profanity after council meetings — NOT for ceremony, statecraft, or commanding Nyxens
- Leofric was terrible at pronunciation; Seraphine laughed at him for it
- Comedy and warmth, not gravity — brothers swearing under their breath in Aurouvian while siblings snort

**Naming convention (ASOIAF rules for "of"):**
- **"FirstName HouseName"** — casual: "Cersei Lannister," "Hilda Dorsen," "Elen Varen"
- **"FirstName of House HouseName"** — formal: "Jaime of House Lannister," "Catelyn of House Rathen." The word "House" is REQUIRED when using "of" with a family name.
- **"FirstName of SeatName"** — refers to the castle/seat, NOT the family: "Arya of Winterfell," "Elyn of Stillford." Valid when emphasising where someone is from.
- **NEVER "FirstName of HouseName"** without "House" — "Hilda of Dorsen" is wrong because it reads as "Hilda of [the castle Dorsen]" and Dorsen is not the castle (Rivermark is). If using "of," always include "House."
- Commoners from villages: "Mera of the Fold," "Bess of Quenby" — fine, they are literally "of" that place.
- Foreign nationals: "Seraphine of Aurouvah" — fine, foreign kingdom.

**Language rule: no anachronistic vocabulary in prose.** Words that did not exist in the medieval period must not appear in narrative text. Known banned words:
- **"biological"** — use "natural" or equivalent
- **"inertia"** — use "the fall," "the weight," "the ground's patience," or rephrase
Existing author-facing notes (e.g. nyxen-origins-and-hide.md) retain technical language where already written, but all new prose and in-world content avoids these terms. Flag any word that sounds post-Renaissance.

**Riders fly solo (NEW):**
- Riders do NOT travel with handlers on missions. Handlers stay at the roost.
- Think Targaryens — no dragonkeepers on campaign. A rider mounts up and goes.
- Oren only travels in Ch2 because he is training the pup, not because riders need keepers on the road.
- Ground escorts are sent ahead separately; the rider flies to meet them.

**ASOIAF plausibility test (NEW):**
- Before writing or keeping any scene/line, put the characters in ASOIAF positions and ask: is this moment plausible? Would a Targaryen do this? Would Tywin say this? If it fails the test, cut it.
- Applied examples: (1) "feed her handlers before he fed me" — a princess being served before a minor kinsman is feudal hierarchy, not a character beat. Cut. (2) Elyra travelling with handlers — Targaryens never set out with dragonkeepers. Cut all handler references from her mission.
