# Southerlins and the hatreds around Southfrost

This builds on `southfrost-frost-glow-and-the-islanders.md` and fits the Southerner look described there (tall, broad, rosy, narrow-nosed, heavy-lidded, oily-haired, dark-eyed, almost never sweating, and with no smell). Placeholder names are marked.

## 1. The word

- **Southerlin** (plural **Southerlins**). A slur for a Southerner. It comes from *Southerling*, an old word for a person of the south, with the small-creature ending of *gosling* and *nestling* turned into a sneer: "little southerners," "things from the south."
- **Who says it.** Only hostile neighbors: Deepwood border folk, the Rivermark pass-folk, and rough Crownfold men near the Winterdoors road. **Polite society says "Southerner."** A lord who says "Southerlin" aloud is insulting the man in front of him on purpose.
- **Who never says it.** Southerners never call themselves that. (A younger generation might take it back in jest later. Do not decide that now.)

## 2. Why the hatred runs so deep

1. **The look.** They seem unearthly to people who sweat and smell. Neighbors say they look "like something carved," "like they were never cold," "fish-eyed" (the heavy lids and dark eyes).
2. **The women.** Many northern and border women are drawn to Southerners because of how they look. The men resent it bitterly. This is the sharpest, most personal cause, and it is why so many tavern fights start.
3. **The smell and the sweat.** A people who do not sweat and do not smell seem unnatural to those who do.
4. **Frost-Glow.** The South has a cheap fuel the rest of the realm pays a fortune for. Envy and a sense of being cheated.
5. **The timber.** Deepwood sold timber to Southfrost for centuries. Frost-Glow ended that, and the lords who grew rich on the old bottleneck blame the Southerners for their loss.
6. **A real feud with a long past.** See below.

## 3. The feud: Dask and Fennick

Placeholder houses. This is the realm's version of an old, venomous neighbor feud over land.

- **House Dask of Skarholt** (Southfrost, western edge) and **House Fennick of Alderfall** (a Deepwood house, a vassal of Selborn at Pinefall) share a border in the high country above **the Weeping Vein**, Skarholt's richest Frost-Glow mine.
- **The boundary stones.** A surveyor of Aldren II set stones along the line four hundred years ago. Each house swears the other moved them in the night. Every generation has found a new reason to prove it. There have been killings over a single stone.
- **What it has become.** Dask men call Fennick men "wet-wool" and "sweatbags." Fennick men call Dask men "Southerlins" and worse. Men of both houses have fought at fairs, in taverns and at wedding feasts.
- **Why it matters.** It gives **Lord Ingvar Dask** (the traitor) a personal reason to hate the north long before the Islanders arrive, and it explains how the Islanders can speak to him as "kin": they look like him, they hate the Deepwood too, and they have never sweated either.
- **The Dask-Fennick feud is the example, not the whole story.** Border folk who have never heard of either house use the same words.

## 4. The insults

**Said to Southerners:** Southerlin, freak, fish-eye, carving, ice-lid, dead-eyes.

**Said by Southerners:** sweatbag, wet-wool, stinkpot, mudfoot, dripper.

**Example:**

> "Oh. It's you Southerlins again. Freaks."
> "Mind your tongue. I am a Southerner, you sweatbag."

## 5. How it touches the rest

- **The Winter Knights** are wanted in a battle line and hated in the tavern afterward. Lords who borrow them learn to billet them apart.
- **Alaric** is half Southern. In Deepwood and the Rivermark pass he will hear **"half-Southerlin."** That slur should come from someone who meets him in person, never from the narrator.
- **Rhaenaya** married into it and finds it funny and sad. She might say the Deepwood foresters spit when they say it and that Osric once had it stitched on a cushion.
- **The Islanders** look like Southerners and share the hatred of the Deepwood. Their offer to Dask lands because of that.
- **The royal court** is mostly above it. The Brennans' own look is unearthly too, and some of the court mutters about that behind a hand.
- **Women.** Never write a woman's attraction as a reason for her to be mocked. Write the men's jealousy as theirs.
