## THE SCENT / BONDING MECHANIC

- The bond is raising and scent, NOT sorcery
- "The scents the Nyxens recognise flows through first children only, with siblings having it weaker"
- "In many cases, third borns and below are unable to ride a Nyxen... because they're too far in birth from the heir"
- The direct line preserves a specific gene cluster that rarely survives intact in side branches
- Royal scholars call it "the firstborn blood" — scientists in a later era would discover that's not actually what's happening (the myth and the science are different things)
- All children of the blood may ride, but further from direct descent = weaker scent
- At each generation, younger children carry less, their children less still, until the scent is too thin

**How it works — the genetics:**

The farmer possessed a rare mutation — a gene cluster causing production of a distinctive protein complex (skin oil / pheromone precursor). The Nyxens evolved to recognize this molecule as "friend." They smell molecules, not family trees. 

**Why birth order matters:**
The scent flows mainly in the first child. It gets weaker the more you go down the line. However, genetics are unpredictable — there are rare anomalies where the scent gets too thin in some older children, but remains abundant enough in the younger ones. 

**Thin Scent & Anomalies:** The scent gets weaker the further down the line from the firstborn. It can be too thin in some children for a normal pup to notice (like Rhaenaya, who cannot ride) and manifest strongly in others further down the line (like Elyra and Gareth). Sometimes anomalies even exist where nieces and nephews of the heir become riders.

**The mechanism is sex-blind.** The scent doesn't care about gender — only about how much protein the person's skin produces. Elyra (5th-born) rides. Edwyn (3rd-born) rides. The realm's LAW says firstborn; the natural order says first child, period. The gap between those two is what makes Seren's story (Book 2) and Elowen's cliffhanger explosive.

**The Brennan Cinder is visible but cosmetic; the curse is invisible.** Two separate Aurouvian magical acts sit on the Brennan bloodline: (1) the Cinder — a harmless cosmetic procedure Leofric chose to match his Nyxen's rare ash-charcoal coat, a rider's tribute made permanent by sorcery; and (2) the blood ritual — the sorcery that transferred the Nyxen heir-scent from Aldric (Leofric's firstborn by Elen of Varen, born at Foldhall) to Seraphine's son, killing Aldric in the process. The scent itself is entirely natural. The blood ritual forced foreign magic onto that natural system, and the sorcerers warned Leofric they were breaking something that could not be unbroken. **The Cinder is NOT the curse.** The scent cannot be faked, cannot be seen, and makes the dynasty's claim rest on whether an animal accepts you. The visible trait (Cinder) is a vain man's love letter to his beast; the invisible trait (scent) is the natural connection that was broken by sorcery and now fights itself every generation.

**Anomalies:** Rare cases of the heir's immediate nieces/nephews being able to ride. Even rarer for more distant relations. For anomaly riders: both the human must inherit an unusually intact version of the trait AND the Nyxen must possess unusually sensitive smell. When both anomalies occur, a connection forms. These riders are paired to ONE Nyxen only. The scent is NOT about intermarriage spreading Brennan blood — strictly about birth order proximity to the firstborn line. The raiser names the beast and receives a call-pipe.

## NYXEN LOYALTY LAYERS

Two overlapping instincts:
1. Recognition of the ancient hereditary marker (scent) from Farmer Brennan
2. Personal attachment built through years of interaction

A Nyxen isn't property or magically bound — it's an intelligent animal with preferences. Nyxens aren't magical soulmates. Just like a Knight might have his trusty horse, won many battles with it, but could hop on another horse anytime anyday and even have his own horse follow along without a leash because they know each other. Any bloodline member can command one, but the response varies: slower, less coordinated, less trusting than with its own raiser. Like "a military dog that recognizes every member of a family but has a favorite handler."

**Veteran Nyxen:** Has decades of habits, instincts, expectations from previous riders. May be stubborn, refuse risky maneuvers, ignore commands it believes foolish.

**Companion Nyxen:** Younger, weaker, but reacts almost as an extension of rider's thoughts from growing up together.

## NYXEN BIRTH

Usually one pup per birth. Twins are rare — spoken of when they happen, remembered for years. Triplets are once-in-a-generation. Litters of four or five pups are legend: maybe twice in recorded Brennan history, talked about the way smallfolk talk about comets.

Pups are born blind, furred, with folded wing-membranes too soft for flight. They nurse for several months, then wean onto fruit mash. By the time they're old enough to be separated from the mother, they're roughly the size of a large hound — clumsy, loud, and unable to lighten reliably.

## THE FREE-ROAMING COMPOUND (How Pairing Works)

Nyxens are **not** confined to the roost. The roost is merely where they sleep or rest. When you enter the compound of Foldhall, you are welcomed by young Nyxen pups freely roaming around the courtyards—joyous, playful, and curious—like peacocks or hounds in an emperor's garden, with wild adult Nyxens flying up above. 

There is no formal "presentation ritual." It works naturally: when a member of the bloodline walks through the compound, if a roaming pup takes an interest in them, approaches, and sticks around, they have a connection. If the pup ignores them, walks away, or acts aggressively, the person cannot have it. 

If a noble tries to force a connection—taking a pup home that didn't choose them—it will end in disaster. When the beast comes of age to fly, it will break all hell loose, destroy its confines, and go feral.

## THE UNNAMED PUP & THE TWINS

**Mother:** Lark (Elyra's Nyxen). **Sire:** Hart (Rodrik's Nyxen). 

**The Pairing Attempts:**
1. **Rowan** (2nd-born) — As a child, he walked the compound looking for a connection, but was simply never accepted by any pup (his scent was too thin, much like his sister Rhaenaya; though a sensitive Nyxen might have accepted him, none ever did). He never tried again as an adult. He has a kingdom's arithmetic to keep, and considers himself too occupied with ledgers to start training a Nyxen pup anyway.
2. **Rhaenaya** (4th-born) — on a visit home, she walks the courtyards where the Unnamed Pup is roaming. She tries to coax it. The pup sniffs her and wanders away. Her scent is too thin. 
3. **Alaric (Rhaenaya's son)** — When Rhaenaya brings her infant son Alaric to visit Foldhall, she takes him out into the compound. The Unnamed Pup ignores the baby completely. (Alaric later proves to be an anomaly rider and pairs with *Frost* from a different litter).

**The Pups:** 
The Unnamed Pup continues to freely roam the Foldhall courtyards, unattached. Then, several more pups are born across the roosts in a relatively short window. Right on the heels of this, King Rodrik rushes home from a campaign to discover his wife has given birth to twin sons (Edric and Harwin). 

Two of the royal pups roaming the compound grow up playing with the twin toddlers in the courtyard, eventually connecting with them to become **Ash** and **Storm**. The other pups born in this window would go on to pair with the cousins born around the same time.

## NYXEN GRIEF & LIFESPAN

Usually die of grief in solitude after their rider's death. Lifespan unknown — no Nyxen has ever died of old age in recorded history; they either die in battle, in mating duels, or of grief. **Oak** = only known grief-survivor (Aldren VII's mount → second rider Aldren VIII; solitary after VIII's death; Harwin fetches in Twin War for promised death).

Grief is about the **rider connection only.** Nyxens do not grieve mates (mating is seasonal, not paired for life — see below). A nursing dam who loses pups may grieve maternally, but this is rare and usually not fatal.

## NYXEN MATING

**Nyxens do not pair for life.** Mating is seasonal and opportunistic. A female becomes receptive every few years (slow reproductive cycle) — quietly, without restless pacing or vocal display. The handlers learn to read the subtle signs (changes in grooming, in where she roosts, in which males she tolerates near her). The strongest male mates with her. After mating, the male returns to his roost and his rider. No lasting attachment. The mother raises the pup alone or with handler support. The sire has no parental role. **Do not describe female Nyxens as “in heat.”** Use “receptive,” “in season,” or “willing.”

**A rider never gains a second Nyxen through mating.** Pups belong to the roost, not to the sire’s or dam’s rider. Mating is the body’s business; the connection is a matter of loyalty.

**Population control:**
1. Male-heavy birth ratio — fewer breeding females
2. Males fight to the death for mating access
3. Slow reproductive cycle — receptive only every few years
4. Low litter size — usually one pup
5. Grief-death after rider loss
6. Late maturation — years to reach breeding age

Handlers manage the roost: receptive females separated if timing is poor, males kept occupied. Mating duels discouraged but not always preventable.
