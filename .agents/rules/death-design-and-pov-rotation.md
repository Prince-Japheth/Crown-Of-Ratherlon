# Death design, fight lethality, POV rotation

## 1. Deaths before the Cliff

The book kills often, including beloved main characters. Lead status gives no protection. Every named death must change what another character can do. The controlling roster and attachment rules are in `brennan-hair-greywater-and-travel.md`, section 5: at least three scenes across two chapters, including one funny scene, with none about the character's end. No doom tags or promise stacks. There is no six-chapter death budget. Never repeat a mechanism; no royal dies by the false-safety pattern.

**Pregnancy unease:** Readers may fear Elyn will die in childbirth and leave Rodrik heartbroken, but she survives the birth. Build that concern through ordinary preparations, distance, and Rodrik’s attachment, without doom tags or fabricated medical emergencies. See the locked thread in `book-outline-2.md`; Elyn’s later Twin War fate is separate.

### Fixed deaths do not protect earlier scenes (locked)

The death roster is author knowledge only. Readers must not be able to infer that a character is safe until a scheduled death. Before those deaths, credible threats may end in survival, injury, capture, retreat, rescue, or lasting loss. Survival must follow established ability, a visible decision, available help, or circumstances prepared in the scene; rank and viewpoint status cannot provide the explanation.

- Use threats that arise from the conflict and location. Do not add gratuitous near-deaths merely to produce anxiety.
- Give danger a concrete mechanism, limited escape routes, and uncertainty consistent with the viewpoint’s knowledge. No magical foresight, withheld facts the viewpoint plainly knows, or surprise rescuer with no route to the scene.
- Carry material consequences when the event warrants them: wounds, exhaustion, damaged equipment, lost ground, obligations, grief, or changed behaviour. Do not require a casualty quota or a lasting injury for every hazard.
- Vary severity and outcome; repeated last-second rescues or false death announcements teach readers the trick. Ordinary warmth and uneventful scenes remain necessary.
- Do not plant farewell speeches, promise stacks, keepsakes, ominous author commentary, or an “overly perfect” loved one as a death marker. Attachment comes from a full person’s life, including flaws and humour.
- During Elyn’s pregnancy, danger can make readers fear either spouse’s premature death. Both survive that period; this does not alter their later fixed fates. See `book-outline-2.md`.

## 2. The false-safety death (pattern)

1. **Build attachment through ordinary life.** Give the character work, appetite and jokes across the required scenes and chapters. Do not plant a doom-shaped promise; any tragic echo belongs afterward.
2. **Real danger first.** The fight must kill other named people on the page and must put the victim under the surface (here, literally) so the reader believes the loss.
3. **Relief.** The victim is pulled out alive. Allow one full scene of warmth, talk and plain joy.
4. **Safety in sight.** Help, a gate or a wall is visible and close. A companion asks to move. The victim declines for one ordinary reason (tiredness, a joke, the sense that the danger has run away north).
5. **One forgotten fact.** A detail from the fight (a wounded man crawling into the alders) is shown and then dropped. The reader should remember it half a line too late.
6. **A small ending.** A sound like a hand on a table. No speech. The chapter stops inside the victim's head.
7. **Consequence.** The death must feed another thread (section 5).

## 3. Lethal fights (checklist for every fight scene)

- Violence must have a credible cost. Do not force a beloved death into every fight’s first third; a required quota telegraphs deaths and conflicts with the attachment ramp.
- The POV character is hurt or nearly killed, and the scene does not pretend otherwise.
- The first danger comes from a quarter the reader was not watching.
- Nyxens are not a shield. Arrows, ropes, pots, wounds and cold still kill riders.
- The fight is shorter than its build-up. The cost is counted after.
- No rider is saved by being a Brennan. Ser, sergeant and smallfolk are as likely to die as a prince.

## 4. Rodrik's death (locked design)

- Rodrik does not die in a charge, a duel or a battle line. The reader has been trained to expect him to walk out of danger (the stag in Ch 6, the Redcut roar, the gully in Ch 1).
- He dies in the Second Ashen War, at an ordinary moment while the realm thinks the campaign is won, doing the thing he always does: stepping in for someone with no rank. A smallfolk child in a burning barn. The roof comes down. No enemy blade, no Nyxen near enough to help, Hart watching from the ground.
- It is seen through a non-Brennan POV, in a few plain paragraphs, in a chapter that opens on something else.
- He dies without having named a Hand (see `world-building-4.md`) and without having named an heir. Ch 14 and 15 concern the Master of Coin; chapter 20 settles that office. The Hand remains empty by policy throughout his reign.
- No chapter before it may show Rodrik as doomed.

## 5. Threads left open that deaths and chapters must feed

- **The five Ashen prisoners and the voice.** Killed in the ford below Harrow Hold with Harrun's escort (Ch 17). The old prisoner's last words ("the same voice, the one from the north road") die with him. The attackers wore Ashen ash over Crownfold boots and spoke Crownfold.
- **The slinger's purse.** The wounded slinger is taken alive or dead by Harrow's riders. He carries new-struck crown silver from one die. Rowan can trace a coin batch through the mint rolls to Barrow and Wend and so to Pell. First tracing chapter: Rowan, after Ch 18.
- **The leak.** The cart's leaving day was known to Pell. Candidates the reader has seen: Fenlow's nephew at Eastgirt's gate (Ch 17), Fenlow's two hundred and twelve with Ser Boremund. Do not resolve before Rowan traces the coin.
- **Dunstan.** Merecroft's steward was killed in an alley by Fenlow's nephew (Ch 9) and nothing has followed. The watch entered it as footpads. Isolde has not told anyone. She tells Edwyn, once, when her brother is out of reach of a letter.
- **Wim and Dunstan** are the pattern: every witness near Pell dies by accident, river or road.
- **The nephew** is unnamed in prose so far. Name him only when he is first called to account.
- **Hamo Merecroft** is dead (Ch 18). His sealed fifth letter is in Rowan’s hands and Isolde is the living witness to Fenlow’s table. Pell’s end is in `brennan-hair-greywater-and-travel.md`, section 5.

## 6. POV rotation (amends `book-outline-1.md`)

The rule "after two royal-family chapters, a non-Brennan POV" was broken by chapters 11 to 15 (Elyra, Elyn, Elyn, Rowan, Edwyn) and the three-chapter run 3, 4, 5 (Elyra, Elyra, Rowan). Ruling:

- Kelwards, Rathens, Varens and other houses count as non-Brennan. Elyn counts as household.
- A run of household POVs may never exceed two. When a birth, death or crisis forces a longer run, the next **three** chapters are outsiders.
- Two chapters in a row from the same POV are allowed only when the second is a direct continuation within one day.
- Chapters 16 (Alton, Kelward) and 17 (Harrun, Crownfold) are outsiders. Chapters 18 and 19 must also be outsiders.
