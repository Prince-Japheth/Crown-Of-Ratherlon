# How the Twin War is built

This is the controlling campaign structure for Parts II–IV, read alongside the outline and the two Twin War dynamics files. The chosen engagements and death placements below are canon. Month windows are approximate operational anchors, not promises to readers.

## 1. Your picture against the real shape of wars

You asked which is more believable: one enormous day with everyone on the field, or many separate engagements. The answer is both, in order.

- **A war of 18 to 24 months** (canon) cannot be one day. Armies march slowly, feed slowly and winter. The Dance of the Dragons is a good model: many engagements in many places, each one costing something different.
- **Armies will not gather in one place unless something forces them.** The great day works only if there is a reason both sides must be there, and neither can leave.
- So the war is a chain of separate engagements, and **the last one is your clash**, set in the one place both sides are compelled to fight over: the **Foldhall roost cliffs**.

## 2. Why the cliffs: the forced convergence

- Foldhall is the capital, but the real prize is the **roosts**. Whoever holds the roost cliffs holds the unbonded Nyxens that live there, the pups, the old mothers and the nursery. A king without the roosts has no sky to speak of.
- Aeion sits inside Foldhall with Elyn, the seal, and the grain. Aeian cannot be crowned anywhere else, because the crown is there and the lords will not accept it from a camp.
- Aeian cannot starve Aeion out (Aeion holds the grain). Aeion cannot let Aeian settle on the plain below, because every week his rival sits there, a noble house defects. Both must fight soon.
- The arrival of **Oak** gives Aeian a reason to strike now, and gives Aeion a reason to meet him before Oak is rested.

That is a believable reason for everything to meet on one field at the end of a long war, not on day one.

## 3. The map problem (from `twin-war-dynamics-1.md`)

- **Aeian** holds two blocs far apart: the **West** (Deepwood and parts of the Merelands) and the **East** (Eastverge and Rivermark, with the Kelwards). Crownfold sits between them and is Aeion's.
- **Aeion** holds the centre: Crownfold, the Vine Shore (Roselyn's house), the coasts and most of the Merelands. He has grain, ships and money.
- **Southfrost** is split: Osric Norvalen and the Winter Knights go to Aeion, and Alaric defects to Aeian.
- Aeian's problem is the vice. His two blocs cannot reach each other except through Aeion's land or by sea. His answer is the air: Nyxens cross the roughly thousand-mile gap in about four flying days, with a rest between sustained runs.

## 4. The engagements (Part III in order)

| 1 | **The Empty Chair** | Months 0 to 3 | Camps form, Roselyn dies, Aderyn defects, Caeylehn disobeys Rowan | Court politics, no battles, but the first real dread |
| 2 | **The Fold Ford** | Month 4 | Aeion's riders ambush a Aeian supply column crossing the Fold. First Nyxen-against-Nyxen fight | The **first family rider death** (see section 6). Nyxen-bone weapons appear |
| 3 | **The Grain Winter** | Months 5 to 9 | Aeion closes the Vine Shore grain and the river tolls. Aeian's East starves. The Rivermark is forced to choose | Smallfolk dying. No heroes in this stretch |
| 4 | **Eastgirt and the Kelward charge** | Months 8 to 11 | Aeian tries to break out of the East with a bront charge. Aeion sends the Winter Knights | The bront line is destroyed. Alton is Duke and has a decision |
| 5 | **The Merelands road** | Months 11 to 14 | Aeian's West strikes at Stillford (Elyn's family home) to pull Aeion out of Foldhall | **Elyra's death** (section 6), a family death for every reader |
| 6 | **Oak** | Months 14 to 18 | Aeian flies north to the high crags and comes back with Oak. Aeion learns of it and must act | The long chapter of waiting. Everyone knows what is coming |
| 7 | **The Cliff** | Months 18 to 24 | The great clash and the final duel | Edwyn, Cassia, the twins, and the unknown number of others |

Each engagement should answer three questions: what does each side want, what goes wrong, and what is lost that cannot be got back.

## 5. Nyxen battle doctrine (your picture, written down)

Use this in every engagement that has riders. It keeps the sky from becoming a mess.

1. **The Shadow.** Riders of the royal family arrive first, in the air, before the ground armies clash. Enemy archers and slingers fire at the riders. Arrows do not hurt a Nyxen's hide, but a rider can be hit through a gap in his armor. The roar is used at once: a spoken Xyrgōs command releases the kill roar against the enemy’s knights before they meet our own in the mix.
2. **Clear the ground beneath the blow.** Repeat roars require a new spoken command each time. Once lines mix, riders cannot roar into the melee without hitting their own people; an isolated enemy position can still be struck.
3. **The Harrying.** The riders then swoop, seize a knight, carry him up and drop him, like an eagle taking snakes, again and again over a large number of enemies.
4. **The King comes down.** The King, and other riders of his choosing, dismount and fight beside their knights. Before leaving the saddle, the rider gives the Nyxen a **standing order** to keep harrying. The Nyxen continues alone until the rider's pipe recalls it. (This keeps the Nyxen obedient: it is carrying out an order, not choosing.)
5. **Not every battle needs the king.** Small matters go to lords and knights. The family rides only when the realm is truly at stake.
6. **Kings die in wars.** Aldren I (sling stone) and Aldren VI (arrow) both did. Do not let any royal rider feel immune.
7. **A pipe calls; it does not confer a bond.** A dead rider’s pipe is retired in the armoury, never inherited or reassigned. A stolen pipe cannot command a beast. Aeian approaches Oak through his own Brennan scent and repeated contact, leaving Storm in a safe roost while he is away.

## 6. Where the planned deaths fall

The following death placements are the selected author-facing design. Do not expose their schedule or use it to make earlier dangers feel safe.

| Death | Where | Notes |
|-------|-------|-------|
| **Simon Kelward** (yours, already locked) | Before the war, the retaking of the Redcut | See `simon-kelward-death.md` |
| **Tristan** (mine) | Engagement 2, the Fold Ford: the first family rider death | A young rider, shot down by Nyxen-bone bolts. This is the event that tells everyone the old taboo has ended. It also makes Edwyn a man with something to avenge, which sets up the cliff |
| **Elyra** (yours, moved) | Engagement 5, the Merelands road | Elyra is on Aeian's side, with the Kelwards. Aderyn has defected to Aeion. During an ambush on a Kelward column she sees her son's Nyxen in the middle of the melee and puts herself between it and a bone-tipped bolt. She dies shielding him in a fight she thought she was winning. This is **before** the cliff, which gives Aderyn a reason to survive it and Alton a reason to hate his son's side |
| **Edwyn** (yours) | The Cliff | Oak seizes him and Briar in mid-air and hurls them in opposite directions. Briar, spinning, cannot get her wings open and strikes the ground. Edwyn is flung into the mountain face |
| **Cassia** (yours) | The Cliff | She is on a wild-tamed Nyxen. She follows her father's fall at full speed, cannot stop in time and dies against the same rock |
| **The twins** (locked) | The Cliff, end | Both twins die. One body returns as a third thing, carrying neither name, and is crowned Aldren X. No resurrection; no later disclosure identifies him as either twin |

**Do not advertise the scheduled deaths.** Each character lives fully, with a future readers can believe in. Credible danger may make readers fear an earlier death; no passage marks a character’s fixed death in advance. See `death-design-and-pov-rotation.md`.

## 7. Why Elyra's death moves earlier

Your picture had Elyra, Edwyn and Cassia dying on the same day. I split them for three reasons:

- If three beloved riders die in one battle, readers feel numb by the third.
- Elyra's death works best when Aderyn is alive and angry afterward. It cannot do that if it happens in the last hour.
- Spreading the deaths across the war is the only way to keep every engagement dangerous. A reader who knows the family's deaths all fall on one day will relax for the first twenty-three months.

## 8. Locked campaign dependencies

1. **Nyxen-bone supply:** Aeian’s Deepwood allies purchase old bone and claw stocks preserved from the hunting era and the timber-thieves’ conflict under Aldren VI. These are relics and illicit stores, not products of an unexplained new slaughter. The Fold Ford battle exposes their use; captured weapons and recovered remains then give Aeion’s side a source as the war escalates. Do not let either faction produce unlimited bone ammunition.
2. **Oak and the pipe:** Aeian approaches Oak through sufficient scent and repeated contact. He leaves Storm in a safe roost for this journey. Dead kings’ pipes stay retired in the armoury; neither inherited metal nor a stolen tone creates the bond. The rider’s own pipe is the summons after acceptance, as section 5 specifies.
3. **Ashen timing:** Firedrakes already appeared during the Opening and fought in the Second Ashen War before the Twin War. The Ashen maintain the recent truce while watching the succession conflict, then exploit the realm’s exhaustion after the cliff. “Stay out until the cliff” refers to the new Ashen intervention, not the beasts’ first appearance.
