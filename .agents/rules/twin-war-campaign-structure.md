# How the Twin War is built

This is a proposal. I took the wheel as you asked. It keeps every beat in `book-outline-1.md` (Parts II to IV) and `twin-war-dynamics-1.md` and `twin-war-dynamics-2.md`, and it keeps your picture of the great clash and the final duel. Names and dates are placeholders.

## 1. Your picture against the real shape of wars

You asked which is more believable: one enormous day with everyone on the field, or many separate engagements. The answer is both, in order.

- **A war of 18 to 24 months** (canon) cannot be one day. Armies march slowly, feed slowly and winter. The Dance of the Dragons is a good model: many engagements in many places, each one costing something different.
- **Armies will not gather in one place unless something forces them.** The great day works only if there is a reason both sides must be there, and neither can leave.
- So the war is a chain of separate engagements, and **the last one is your clash**, set in the one place both sides are compelled to fight over: the **Foldhall roost cliffs**.

## 2. Why the cliffs: the forced convergence

- Foldhall is the capital, but the real prize is the **roosts**. Whoever holds the roost cliffs holds the unbonded Nyxens that live there, the pups, the old mothers and the nursery. A king without the roosts has no sky to speak of.
- Edric sits inside Foldhall with Elyn, the seal, and the grain. Harwin cannot be crowned anywhere else, because the crown is there and the lords will not accept it from a camp.
- Harwin cannot starve Edric out (Edric holds the grain). Edric cannot let Harwin settle on the plain below, because every week his rival sits there, a noble house defects. Both must fight soon.
- The arrival of **Oak** gives Harwin a reason to strike now, and gives Edric a reason to meet him before Oak is rested.

That is a believable reason for everything to meet on one field at the end of a long war, not on day one.

## 3. The map problem (from `twin-war-dynamics-1.md`)

- **Harwin** holds two blocs far apart: the **West** (Deepwood and parts of the Merelands) and the **East** (Eastverge and Rivermark, with the Kelwards). Crownfold sits between them and is Edric's.
- **Edric** holds the centre: Crownfold, the Vine Shore (Roselyn's house), the coasts and most of the Merelands. He has grain, ships and money.
- **Southfrost** is split: Osric Norvalen and the Winter Knights go to Edric, and Alaric defects to Harwin.
- Harwin's problem is the vice. His two blocs cannot reach each other except through Edric's land or by sea. His answer is the air: Nyxens can cross the gap in three days.

## 4. The engagements (Part III in order)

| # | Engagement | When | What each side wants | What it costs |
|---|------------|------|----------------------|---------------|
| 1 | **The Empty Chair** | Months 0 to 3 | Camps form, Roselyn dies, Aderyn defects, Caelen disobeys Rowan | Court politics, no battles, but the first real dread |
| 2 | **The Fold Ford** | Month 4 | Edric's riders ambush a Harwin supply column crossing the Fold. First Nyxen-against-Nyxen fight | The **first family rider death** (see section 6). Nyxen-bone weapons appear |
| 3 | **The Grain Winter** | Months 5 to 9 | Edric closes the Vine Shore grain and the river tolls. Harwin's East starves. The Rivermark is forced to choose | Smallfolk dying. No heroes in this stretch |
| 4 | **Eastgirt and the Kelward charge** | Months 8 to 11 | Harwin tries to break out of the East with a bront charge. Edric sends the Winter Knights | The bront line is destroyed. Alton is Duke and has a decision |
| 5 | **The Merelands road** | Months 11 to 14 | Harwin's West strikes at Stillford (Elyn's family home) to pull Edric out of Foldhall | **Elyra's death** (section 6), a family death for every reader |
| 6 | **Oak** | Months 14 to 18 | Harwin flies north to the high crags and comes back with Oak. Edric learns of it and must act | The long chapter of waiting. Everyone knows what is coming |
| 7 | **The Cliff** | Months 18 to 24 | The great clash and the final duel | Edwyn, Cassia, the twins, and the unknown number of others |

Each engagement should answer three questions: what does each side want, what goes wrong, and what is lost that cannot be got back.

## 5. Nyxen battle doctrine (your picture, written down)

Use this in every engagement that has riders. It keeps the sky from becoming a mess.

1. **The Shadow.** Riders of the royal family arrive first, in the air, before the ground armies clash. Enemy archers and slingers fire at the riders. Arrows do not hurt a Nyxen's hide, but a rider can be hit through a gap in his armor. The roar is used at once: a storm-force blast bursts the helmets and heads of the enemy's knights before they meet our own in the mix.
2. **The roar is used only once.** After the lines meet, a rider cannot roar again without killing his own knights. This is why the first blast is decisive.
3. **The Harrying.** The riders then swoop, seize a knight, carry him up and drop him, like an eagle taking snakes, again and again over a large number of enemies.
4. **The King comes down.** The King, and other riders of his choosing, dismount and fight beside their knights. Before leaving the saddle, the rider gives the Nyxen a **standing order** to keep harrying. The Nyxen continues alone until the rider's pipe recalls it. (This keeps the Nyxen obedient: it is carrying out an order, not choosing.)
5. **Not every battle needs the king.** Small matters go to lords and knights. The family rides only when the realm is truly at stake.
6. **Kings die in wars.** Aldren I (sling stone) and Aldren VI (arrow) both did. Do not let any royal rider feel immune.
7. **The pipe is the weak point.** A riderless Nyxen without a pipe is the most dangerous thing on the field. Whoever holds a dead rider's pipe holds that Nyxen. (Optional idea: Oak's pipe belonged to Aldren VIII, passed to Rodrik, and is the object the twins quarrel over.)

## 6. Where the planned deaths fall

Marked **yours** are deaths you told me you want. **Mine** are proposals you can overrule.

| Death | Where | Notes |
|-------|-------|-------|
| **Simon Kelward** (yours, already locked) | Before the war, the retaking of the Redcut | See `simon-kelward-death.md` |
| **Tristan** (mine) | Engagement 2, the Fold Ford: the first family rider death | A young rider, shot down by Nyxen-bone bolts. This is the event that tells everyone the old taboo has ended. It also makes Edwyn a man with something to avenge, which sets up the cliff |
| **Elyra** (yours, moved) | Engagement 5, the Merelands road | Elyra is on Harwin's side, with the Kelwards. Aderyn has defected to Edric. During an ambush on a Kelward column she sees her son's Nyxen in the middle of the melee and puts herself between it and a bone-tipped bolt. She dies shielding him in a fight she thought she was winning. This is **before** the cliff, which gives Aderyn a reason to survive it and Alton a reason to hate his son's side |
| **Edwyn** (yours) | The Cliff | Oak seizes him and Briar in mid-air and hurls them in opposite directions. Briar, spinning, cannot get her wings open and strikes the ground. Edwyn is flung into the mountain face |
| **Cassia** (yours) | The Cliff | She is on a wild-tamed Nyxen. She follows her father's fall at full speed, cannot stop in time and dies against the same rock |
| **The twins** (yours) | The Cliff, end | The last duel in the sky settles it. One body comes back. The survivor never tells |

**Do not foreshadow any of this.** Each character should live fully on the page, with a future the reader believes in. No scene before the war should make the reader wonder whether they will die.

## 7. Why Elyra's death moves earlier

Your picture had Elyra, Edwyn and Cassia dying on the same day. I split them for three reasons:

- If three beloved riders die in one battle, readers feel numb by the third.
- Elyra's death works best when Aderyn is alive and angry afterward. It cannot do that if it happens in the last hour.
- Spreading the deaths across the war is the only way to keep every engagement dangerous. A reader who knows the family's deaths all fall on one day will relax for the first twenty-three months.

## 8. Three things to check before writing Part III

1. **Who holds the Nyxen-bone supply?** The weapons in `twin-war-dynamics-2.md` need bones, and the bones need a source. Suggestion: the Deepwood timber-thieves of Aldren VI's day kept a store of Nyxen bone from the bodies of beasts that died in the old wars, and one side buys it.
2. **Oak and the pipe.** Decide who rides Oak and who holds the pipe (section 5, point 7).
3. **The Ashen.** The war is the opportunity the Ashen have waited for. Decide whether firedrakes appear early in the Twin War (an opportunist raid on the East) or only after. My recommendation is that they stay out until the Cliff is over. See `ashen-wars-and-firedrakes.md`.
