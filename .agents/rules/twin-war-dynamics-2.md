## THE ASSASSINATION OF LADY ROSELYN VAREN

The event that irreversibly locks the realm into the Twin War is the assassination of Lady Roselyn Varen (Aeion's fiancée). While it is never explicitly confirmed in the text that Aeian ordered the murder, it is universally believed to be his doing for four unshakeable reasons:

1. **The "First Heir" Rumor:** After King Aldren IX dies without naming a successor, a massive rumor spreads: whichever twin sires a legitimate son first will win the crown. The rumor was based on what people thought the Queen (Elyn) would say or do, though it was never confirmed. Because Aeion is deeply in love and on the verge of marrying Roselyn, he has a massive headstart. While Aeian could technically marry a lesser noble quickly to catch up on time, Roselyn is the key to the wealthy Vine Shore. The people believe Aeian eliminated her to not only reset the clock, but to proactively shatter Aeion's most powerful political alliance.
2. **Aeian's Philosophy:** The realm knows Aeian believes "fear is the most reliable foundation for peace" and "compromise is weakness." Murdering a rival's bride to preemptively win a kingdom aligns perfectly with the brutal, pragmatic border tactics he learned in the Eastverge.
3. **Crippling the Diplomat:** Roselyn is not just a girl; she is a Varen, representing the wealthy Vine Shore. Killing her shatters Aeion's happiness and threatens his most powerful political alliance.
4. **The Suspicious Silence:** Aeian's response to the assassination seals his guilt in the public eye: he never denies it, but he never accepts it either. In his court, the murder is simply never mentioned—as if it didn't happen. Aeian weaponizes the ambiguity, knowing that if people are terrified of him, they are less likely to join Aeion.

Because of this terrifying silence, no one bothers looking for another suspect. The rumor becomes treated as absolute fact (driving characters like Aderyn Kelward into rebellion), even though the reader will never know if Aeian truly gave the order.



**The veteran Nyxen problem:** The veteran Nyxen Aeian seeks (Oak) has fought armies — never another Nyxen. Experience still matters but not as much as people think. The prince who values coordination (with his own Nyxen) may accidentally possess the more useful asset.

**Social fracture:** Before the war, riders were one extended brotherhood — trained, flew, hunted, celebrated together. Many riders on opposite sides grew up together. A cousin on one side may have flown formation with a cousin on the other for twenty years. That emotional fracture is more compelling than the military one.

## NYXEN-KILLING TABOO

The kingdom's fear is NOT "can a Nyxen be killed?" Everyone knows they can. The fear is: "What happens after you kill one?"

**The precedent:** The last time a Nyxen was killed in war (Aldren VI's first Nyxen, in the Deepwood), the rider who was king at that time intentionally tracked the killer, took him hostage, and subjected him to days of starvation and excruciating torture, then returned the remains. Since then, every soldier fears harming a Nyxen — they'd rather die in war than live to face a grieving rider's vengeance.

**Cultural taboo:** Songs, folktales, military superstitions. Mothers tell sons: "If you find yourself beneath a Nyxen, pray the rider kills you." Commanders order: "Bring it down if you must. But pray someone else fired the shot."

## HOW NYXENS CAN BE KILLED

NO dedicated "Nyxen-killer weapon" (that mistake makes them stop dominating warfare). Instead, intelligent tactics and weapons fashioned from Nyxen bone are used. The first Nyxens were killed without these, until enough Nyxen bones were in circulation for hunters and armies to craft weapons capable of cutting their thick hide:

- **Wing damage (cumulative):** Inflicted using arrows or blades tipped with Nyxen bone. One tear = still flies. Three tears = reduced maneuverability. Five major tears = cannot perform aggressive turns. Heavily damaged wing during high-speed dive = loss of control. Long tears toward wing root or torn leading edge are catastrophic.
- **Weighted nets / entanglement:** Engineered military nets from siege engines. Entangled during a dive → gravity becomes the enemy.
- **Suffocation:** Gas/fume bombs thrown into dens/caves; fumes suffocate to death.
- **Impact:** Trapped mid-air (nets + fire), can't flap, drops from sky; impact kills.
- **Acoustic disruption:** Large battlefield horns/drums designed to interfere with spatial awareness — scatter formations, prevent coordinated attack runs.

Nyxen hide cannot be pierced by any human blade. Only Nyxen claws and teeth can puncture Nyxen hide (and even that is difficult — requires the beast's own strength). Nyxen bone/claw is sharp enough to cut through hide, making it workable.

## THE CLIFF ENDING

Both twins clash on their Nyxens in a bloody battle. Something sets them ablaze; the fight passes beyond the cliff and both beasts die. Both twins die on the cliff. One body returns, carrying neither name: a third thing, crowned Aldren X. Neither prince is resurrected, and Aldren X must never be resolved as either surviving twin.

The realm reads the returning body as a mirror: each faction sees its own prince. This political projection does not establish that either twin survived. The loss of both persons is the ending's cost. See `brennan-hair-greywater-and-travel.md`, section 4.

## COUSIN INTERACTIONS (Fire & Blood style)

Twins growing up around: Rowan's Caeylehn (who faces the obey-or-disobey choice), Edwyn's presence as the performer uncle, Elyra as the wild aunt who probably sees the warrior twin's coldness before anyone admits it. These dynamics feed the Twin War escalation — loyalties seeded in childhood before anyone draws steel.

**How to apply:** When writing later chapters, the warrior twin's coldness must never be explained by circumstance. Gareth is met in Book 1 mostly by reputation (he's on cold roads, absent) — reader meets the template before the copy.


## REBEL RELATIVES (The Fractured Alliances)
While a region and its paramount head may officially declare for one prince, the relatives of the twin princes—and individual riders or lesser vassals—can and will rebel against that declaration and fight for the side they choose.
Both absolute loyalty and familial rebellion happen simultaneously. A Duke cannot force every son, cousin, or sworn sword to march the same path, especially when both twins have legitimate claims.

**1. Alaric Norvalen (The Southfrost Rebellion):**
- **The Region:** Duke Osric Norvalen declares for **Aeion** (Diplomatic). He looks at his massive mountain walls and wants to keep Southfrost out of the bloodshed.
- **The Rebel:** His son, **Alaric Norvalen** (riding *Frost*).
- **The Reason:** Alaric has spent his youth patrolling the freezing "cold roads" and fighting off wild mountain reavers alongside his Uncle Gareth, and fights beside Aeian in the Frostglow War. Because Alaric actually bleeds on the icy frontier, he respects Aeian's grit and thinks Aeion is too soft. Alaric openly defies his father, mounts *Frost*, and flies north to join Aeian's war camp.

**2. Caeylehn Brennan (The Steward's Rebellion):**
- **The Father:** Rowan is the ambitious ledger-keeper who orders his household to remain cautious or neutral to survive.
- **The Rebel:** His son, **Caeylehn Brennan** (riding *Rook*).
- **The Reason:** Caeylehn is young, idealistic, and disgusted by his father's self-serving politics. He truly believes in Aeion’s diplomatic vision for a unified realm. Caeylehn disobeys his father's strict orders, taking *Rook* to fight for Aeion.

**3. Princess Elyra (The Love Match):**
- **The Stance:** Elyra (riding *Lark*) is happily married to Alton Kelward of the Eastverge. Living in the dusty borderlands, she sees the constant threat of the Ashen Kingdom. Because she loves her husband and understands the survival needs of the Eastverge, she stands fiercely with House Kelward and officially declares for **Aeian**.

**4. Aderyn Kelward (The Idealist's Rebellion):**
- **The Rebellion:** Aderyn is the son of Elyra and Alton Kelward. However, he is a young idealist. When Aeian is universally blamed for the assassination of Lady Roselyn Varen, Aderyn crosses a moral line. He refuses to support a man he believes is a kinslayer/murderer of innocent women, even if he agrees the realm needs strength. He takes his Nyxen, defects from his parents' region, and flies to join **Aeion**.

**5. Edwyn's Bastards (The Secret Bonds):**
- **Tristan (The Noble Bastard):** Edwyn’s firstborn son, born outside marriage, is kept at court but treated as lesser. As Rodrik’s immediate nephew, he is an eligible anomaly: his scent is strong enough for Ysehnys to accept him. He secretly bonds the roost Nyxen and names him from his father’s Aurouvian tongue despite knowing little to nothing of the language; he learns the rider commands by repetition. **The name is also a deliberate jab at those opposing his recognition:** he gives his beast an Aurouvian name from his father’s tongue while they try to deny him a rider’s place. Tristan neither bears an Aurouvian given name nor speaks the language well, yet deliberately chooses it for his beast. He knowingly borrows an existing name rather than coins one. **The central dispute is recognition:** opponents object to entering a bastard on the rider roll and granting him an ordinal and a call-pipe. Edwyn insists that Tristan is his son and a proven rider, entitled to the same recognition and instrument as any other accepted rider. The beast’s acceptance has already settled whether he can ride; his birth does not disqualify him from the roll. Edwyn secures his entry and call-pipe. This is recognition of an existing bond, not royal permission that creates it, and does not itself legitimize Tristan or grant succession rights. Tristan’s exact ordinal remains unset. He later fights for Aeion.
- **Cassia (The Hidden Daughter):** Edwyn is secretly a devoted father who hid his commoner daughter away to protect her from court shame, but visits her often. Cassia knows her blood and manages to tame a wild Nyxen outside the roost system.
- **The Result:** When the war breaks out, Edwyn brings both his bastards to the Diplomatic Prince's side, suddenly granting Aeion's faction three riders from a single unexpected branch.
