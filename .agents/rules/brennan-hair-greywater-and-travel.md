# Hair, Greywater, travel and deaths

## 1. Hairstyles

Brennans wear their hair long and braided. A rider braids one tuft of his Nyxen's coat into his own hair, so the beast is visible on the rider. Hart's tawny-gold shows in Rodrik's; Briar's slate-brown shows in Edwyn's.

- **Rodrik:** long ash-charcoal hair worn loose and wild when he works. At court, two thin temple braids joined behind with a gold ring.
- **Elyn:** the Rathen coronet braid, pinned with silver, worn under the circlet.
- **Rowan:** pulled back severely and tied with black cord. No braid, since he rides nothing.
- **Edwyn:** combed to the shoulder, one beaded braid at the left temple holding Briar's tuft.
- **Elyra:** one thick braid down the back, bound with leather (fixes chapter 1).
- **Gareth:** tied at the nape, unbraided, a frontier boy's hair, with Thorn's grey in a single plait over one ear.
- **Rhaenaya:** coiled plaits pinned ear to ear in the Southfrost fashion.
- **Children:** first braid at four years, cut at no age. A baby's hair is left loose.

## 2. House Greywater (the Aurouvian cadet branch)

- Greywater is the Velaryon of this book: kin to the throne from the far past, speaking the same tongue, always of service to the crown. First on page in chapter 19 (Lord Tomas Brennan, Warden of the Roosts).
- **Twin War side: Aeion.** Reasons: the Greywater duty is to the roosts and the crown seat, and Aeion holds Foldhall; Aeion listens to the Warden and asks the handlers' counsel while Aeian treats them as tools; and their Aurouvian lets them overhear Aeian's camp, which Tomas uses once and regrets. They ride no Nyxens and fight little, but the handlers follow Tomas, and handlers decide whether a rider's beast is fed.
- **Brenna Brennan** is Tomas’s niece and heir, a distant cadet cousin and a non-rider. She does not bond Lark and Hart’s daughter. Greywater has no riders; Brenna serves the roosts through stewardship and handler work.

## 3. No teleportation

Every journey is shown, or given a route, a mode and a time in one line. No scene cut may place a person somewhere they could not have reached at the speeds in `geography-and-logistics.md`. Ravens, relays and flights must be timed everywhere.

The supplied review reports an audit of chapters 1 to 18 with none found. This records that review's finding, not a new audit of the local chapters.

## 4. The twins' fate

Both twins die on the cliff. What returns is a third thing: one body, carrying neither name, crowned Aldren X. That is the book's Daenerys, the end of the person the realm thought it was fighting for. Nobody is resurrected anywhere in the book, ever. Do not resolve Aldren X as either surviving twin or depict a revival of either prince.

## 5. Deaths (replaces the budget line in `death-design-and-pov-rotation.md`)

- The book kills often and kills people readers love, main characters included. The reader must never be safe because a character is a lead.
- **No doom tags.** Never introduce a character with a ticking clock, a keepsake, a promise stack, or a "when this is over" speech to mark them for death. Give them work, appetite and jokes. The tragic echo comes only afterward.
- **Attachment ramp:** for a consequential death staged as a personal loss, at least three living scenes across at least two chapters, at least one funny, none about their end. This applies to Hamo, Harrun, Simon, Dunstan, Wim, Fenn, Cadd and Oswin as foregrounded losses. Historical dead and incidental battlefield casualties do not receive artificial pre-death introductions.
- Never repeat a mechanism. Vary: victory-death, false-safety, flight-to-further-safety (Hamo, Ch 18), door-hold, poison, trial by combat, execution, betrayal at a feast, accident of mercy (Rodrik).
