# Geography and logistics

Measured from `map_compressed.jpg` (2048 by 1265 pixels). Read this before writing or planning any march, siege, flight, blockade or war. If a conflict cannot be explained with a route from this file, change the conflict, not the map.

## 1. Scale (decision, consistent with the LOCKED scale in `world-building-1.md`)

- **1 pixel is about 1.42 miles in a straight line.** The full map is about **2,900 miles** across, which matches "approx. 3,000 miles across."
- Roads wind. **Road distance is about 1.2 times the straight line.**
- Foldhall to Eastgirt is about 770 miles straight and about 920 by road. That is the "roughly 1,000 miles" of the lock, and it gives the lock's **60 days** of infantry at 15 miles a day.
- The lock says "Foldhall to the Eastverge border." Read that as **the Eastverge's eastern frontier with the Ashen Kingdom**, about 880 miles straight and about 1,050 by road. The Eastverge begins much nearer Foldhall, about 320 miles away.

## 4. Borders (who can reach whom by land)

- **Crownfold** touches Vine Shore (north), Merelands and Deepwood (west), Eastverge and Rivermark (east), and Southfrost (south). It is the hub, so whoever holds it can move forces from any region to any other region.
- **Vine Shore** touches Western Shore, Crownfold, and the northern sea. It does **not** touch the Eastverge by land. A sea bay separates them.
- **Western Shore** touches Vine Shore, Merelands, Vaelcross (a short frontier) and the sea.
- **Merelands** touches Western Shore, Vine Shore (briefly), Crownfold, Deepwood and Vaelcross.
- **Deepwood** touches Merelands, Crownfold, Southfrost (across a mountain range) and Vaelcross.
- **Southfrost** touches Deepwood, Crownfold and **Rivermark** (by a pass at its north-east corner). All its other sides are sea.
- **Rivermark** touches Crownfold, Eastverge, Southfrost and the **Ashen Kingdom**.
- **Eastverge** touches Crownfold, Rivermark, the Ashen Kingdom and the northern sea.
- **Only the Eastverge and the Rivermark border the Ashen Kingdom.** Only the Merelands, Western Shore and Deepwood border Vaelcross.

## 5. Rivers and sea lanes

- **The Fold** runs west to east through Crownfold past Foldhall and on north-east to the northern bay. Timber from the Merelands and Deepwood floats down it to Foldhall (which is why Fenlow's timber and Timber Row are where they are). A boat going from Foldhall to Rosegate goes **downriver to the bay and then west along the coast**, not "upriver."
- **The northern bay** lies between the Vine Shore and the Eastverge. Ships from Rosegate reach the Eastverge's north coast in about six days, then it is about 250 miles overland to Eastgirt. That is the realm's fastest way of moving grain to the east, and Vine Shore ships control it.
- **The Rivermark** has a loop of rivers that runs out of the Ashen mountains. It feeds the Rivermark and is a highway for any enemy that enters.
- **The Icewide** is the sea along the south. Pack ice makes it hard to cross, but not impossible for small ships.
- **Wrenholm**, the northern island at the top centre of the map, lies north of the Vine Shore. It is directly held by the crown; do not call it northwestern or unnamed.

## 7. Feasibility audit of every conflict in canon

For each, the route and the time, so no battle is impossible.

### Past

- **Great Civil War (before Aldren I):** Everywhere, long and slow. No audit needed.
- **Oath Winter** (Vine Shore): Holdouts near Rosegate, a short march from the capital. Fine.
- **Dorsen Water War** (Rivermark): River lords fighting over weirs. Fine.
- **Three Dukes' Quarrel** (Dorsen, Kelward, Selborn): Rivermark and the Eastverge touch, so Dorsen and Kelward can fight directly. **Selborn of Deepwood is 980 miles from the Rivermark and cannot march there.** Selborn can only join by hiring ships, sending knights through Crownfold with a king's leave, or acting through proxies. Record that Selborn's part was a timber and money war, not a march.
- **Pirate Wars** (Tarnell of the Western Shore against Varen of the Vine Shore): They touch, and the sea is theirs. Fine.
- **The Reed Summer** (Vaelcross frontier): The Merelands and Deepwood border Vaelcross. Fine.
- **The Bread Riots of the Fold:** Foldhall itself.
- **First Ashen War:** The Ashen came through the Eastverge, which borders them. Aldren VIII was in the far west, **about 1,300 miles from Eastgirt by air, a flight of about 5 days with rests**. He arrived late, which suits the cause file, and he arrived by air. The ground armies could not have, so Rodrik's vanguard in chapter 2 had to be **Kelward and Rivermark forces, not Foldhall infantry**.
- **Aldren VI's death in the Deepwood** and the timber thieves: Deepwood borders Vaelcross, so thieves crossing the frontier is natural.

### Present

- **The Eastverge troubles and the Redcut:** Ashen bands cross the frontier and reach the Redcut in about two days. **Harrun’s initial twenty watch reinforcements travel mounted**, about 30 road miles a day along the kingsroad via Eastgirt, with three packhorses and paid bread/fodder stops rather than a wagon train. About 31 days to Eastgirt and 34 to the outer watch/Redcut; they serve on foot after arrival. This does not change the 60-day schedule for the later five-hundred-man foot reinforcement. Chapter 2 is day 20 after Harrun’s departure; Elyra departs on day 21 and reaches the outer watch about day 24 after three flying days, overtaking the riders en route. Her report estimates Harrun’s arrival in ten days. Chapter 4’s ninth day, counting Elyra’s arrival as day one, is about day 32; Harrun arrives two evenings later, about day 34, before the ten further days leading into chapter 10’s council and the battle. **Elyra's flight** takes about 3 days (matches chapter 3). **A relay rider** takes about 11 days (matches chapter 12). **A raven** takes about 3 days (matches chapter 12).
- **Gareth's three-day flight** to the Redcut (chapter 4) works only if he starts from the **eastern cold roads**, near the pass that joins Southfrost and the Rivermark. From Frostholt it would take four days. Chapter 4's "weeks on the ice with Osric's patrols" can be read as the eastern roads.
- **The harvest-feast Vine Shore grain** (chapters 5, 9, 14): Vine Shore to Foldhall is about 430 road miles, 29 days for wagons. Its wagons enter Foldhall by the **north** gate, not the south (fix in chapter 14).

### Future

- **The Opening and the fire years:** Firedrakes appear in the Ashen mountains. They can reach only the **Eastverge and the Rivermark** by land. The winged ones could reach Foldhall in about three days, but not without being seen. Every attack must fall first on those two regions.
- **The Frostglow War:** Islanders sail from the south-west, about 400 miles to Skarholt. The **Winter Knights hold the passes**. Foldhall to Skarholt is 580 miles, under two days for a Nyxen, so Gareth and Aeian can reach the war quickly and an army cannot (about 50 days).
- **The Second Ashen War:** Fought in the Eastverge and the Rivermark. Supplies come by sea from Vine Shore to the Eastverge's north coast, and by road from Foldhall.
- **The Twin War:** See the next section.

## 8. The Twin War on this map

- **Aeian's two blocs cannot reach each other.** West (Merelands, Deepwood) and East (Eastverge, Rivermark) are about 1,000 miles apart in a straight line, 1,300 by road, 85 days of infantry, and Crownfold lies between them.
- **That is a pincer, not a flaw.** Each bloc is only 40 to 60 days from Foldhall. **If Aeian times his two armies to arrive together, Aeion is attacked from both sides.** Aeion's answer is **interior lines**: he sits at the centre and can move his own host to either front in a month, which neither of Aeian's blocs can do. That is the strategic story of Part III.
- **Why they converge on Foldhall:** Because both arms of the pincer end there, the clash on the roost cliffs is geography, not coincidence.
- **The air is Aeian's shortcut.** Nyxens cross the 1,000-mile gap in about 4 days. Aeian's riders can unite his two blocs for intelligence, raids and signalling, and Aeion's can do the same inside the centre.
- **The grain:** Aeion controls the Vine Shore and Western Shore coasts and ships. He can **blockade the northern bay**, which cuts Aeian's East off from the Vine Shore grain. The Eastverge and Rivermark then depend on their own harvests and the river system, which is what the "Grain Winter" in `twin-war-campaign-structure.md` needs.
- **Southfrost** touches the Rivermark through its north-east pass, so Alaric's defection and a Winter Knight column needs about forty-nine infantry days from the east pass to Eastgirt, or about twenty-five mounted days, plus its journey to the pass. It also sits across Aeian's southern flank.

## 9. Rules for future chapters

1. **Before writing any campaign or battle, write the route.** Name the places the army passes, the days it takes, and who feeds it.
2. **No war between two regions that do not touch** unless you can say what lies between them and what the army does about it.
3. **Letters, ravens, riders and Nyxens** must use the times in section 2.
4. **Do not invent a distance** that contradicts the table. If a chapter needs a different distance, update this file.

## 10. Decisions

1. The eastern icon is Eastgirt (chapters already use it).
2. The northern island is Wrenholm, a smugglers' and pirates' isle tied to the Pirate Wars, taken into the crown by Rodrik's treaty.
3. Elyn's Rathens: Duke Edmund of Stillford declares for Aeion, so Aeian's West is split. A cadet line of Rathens (the Pellridge branch) holds for Aeian and strikes at Stillford in the Merelands engagement, which is why Elyra dies on that road (see `twin-war-campaign-structure.md`).

## Foldhall lanes (chapter 18)

**Chandlers’ Row:** the lane down from the castle gate. Chapter 18’s route passes through Timber Row and a sawpit to the river stair and customs wharf, where Rowan waits.

## Local and timber journeys established in chapter 19

Greywater to Foldhall: about thirty road miles, one day by horse or two by litter, following the upper Fold road south. Alderfall timber reaches the upper Fold by Deepwood forest lanes and then travels downriver to Foldhall; Ralf’s first-thaw raft spends eleven moving days on the river plus four held by ice. This is an upper-river timber route, not a claim that Alderfall is on the sea or that a frozen river can be navigated. Within Foldhall, roost yard to counting-room takes under a quarter hour by the yard stair and lesser-hall passage. Chapter 19 is six days after chapter 18; no long-distance witness reaches Foldhall instantly.

## Opening-year scene chronology (review anchor)

Y0 remains Rodrik’s coronation. Chapter 1 is in the first season of his reign, high summer, roughly two months after coronation; it is not the coronation day. The first twenty reinforcements depart in chapter 1. Chapter 2 follows twenty days later; Elyra departs the next morning, after three weeks. Her flight takes about three days. Harrun arrives about day 34 after his departure, as recorded above.

Chapter 4 shows the first watch duties and first discovery of hunting ropes. Chapters 5–9 carry the parallel household thread into Elyn’s fifth month and the harvest season. Chapter 10 returns to the border in autumn after the intervening patrol weeks; its ten-night council is the new concentrated enemy pressure, not the whole time Elyra or Harrun have been at the watch. Chapter 11 is the next dawn. Chapter 12’s rider reports that battle after eleven days; Rodrik flies east, while Boremund’s previously mustered reserve finally leaves on foot. The later chapter 12 household scenes are ten weeks after that departure; Alaric is then about three months old, and the twins are born in chapter 13 at roughly Y0+8 months. These anchors preserve the existing birth-age table.

Chapters 14–15 are the twins’ forty-day feast and following days. Rodrik remains at Foldhall while waiting for the thaw to open the ground approach to the counterattack; chapter 16 is his return to the Eastverge, four days after his new arrival, not his original chapter 12 arrival. Chapters 17–18 follow the escort journey and the return of news to Foldhall; chapter 19 is six days after Hamo’s death, with twins about four months old. Only interleaving household/border intervals are compressed; do not insert a multi-year skip.

The chapter 5 troop figure is a contingency estimate and a muster order. Provisioning/dismissal authority is not a claim that the reserve already marched in summer. Chapter 12 authorizes departure, and chapter 14 reports its approximately sixty-day journey.
