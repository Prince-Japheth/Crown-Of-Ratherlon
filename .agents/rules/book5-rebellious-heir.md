**Book 5 — post-Twin War era (Year ~600–700?)**

**The Rebellion of the Firstborn’s Line:**

**Part 1 — The Flight:**
Aldren X’s great-grandson is heir to the throne. He refuses it. His argument:
- He didn’t choose this. The crown is a burden, not a gift.
- "If we rule by the fear people have of the creatures we control, then we shouldn’t rule at all."
- He will not have a child born into forced responsibility.
- His parents argue: you are firstborn, the realm is held together by House Brennan’s Nyxen control, you will break that.
- He flees on his Nyxen to another continent. His younger brother is crowned king.

**Part 2 — The Decay:**
Over generations after the firstborn flees:
- In Ratherlon: House Brennan slowly loses Nyxen control (the scent moved with the firstborn line). The sitting kings rule without the bond.
- On the foreign continent: the rebellious son’s descendants bear his name (not Brennan), speak another language, forget the bond’s true nature. They live without using Nyxens for war, don’t know about the roar/sonic scream capability. The family falls into poverty. Only tales remain of once being kings.

**Part 3 — The Return:**
One firstborn descendant, sick of poverty and tales, decides to reclaim the throne:
- "We were once kings. I am its rightful heir by birth order. I am the head that controls the Nyxens now."
- He flies back to Ratherlon.
- War breaks out. The realm splits:
  - **Pro-returnee houses:** He rides the Nyxen. He IS the firstborn line. The bond proves it. This is what made the Brennans kings — not a crown, but the beasts.
  - **Pro-sitting-king houses:** The second son was crowned when the first fled. He didn’t abandon his duty. His line stayed, ruled, bled for Ratherlon. A coronation is a coronation. You don’t get to leave and come back.

**Central question:** What is legitimacy? Blood/bond (biological right, the original source of Brennan power) or crown/duty (institutional right, the thing built by those who stayed)?

**How to apply:** This is the furthest-future book. It retroactively gives weight to every succession crisis in earlier books — the Twin War, Seren’s queenship, even Brennan’s original sheltering of the Nyxens. Every book asks "who deserves to rule?" and this one asks it loudest.
