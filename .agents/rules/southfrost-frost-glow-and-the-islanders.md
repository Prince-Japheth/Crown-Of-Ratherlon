## 2. Frost-Glow

- A dense, luminous, crystalline moss that grows in the hot pockets deep under the southern mountains. It burns with a steady, intense **blue flame**, far longer than charcoal, with almost no smoke and **no smell**.
- Harvested by southern smallfolk glow-diggers under the Norvalen charter, paid by the basket. Mining it is dangerous: bad air and collapses.
- In the South it is a plain necessity. Hearths in Frostholt's lower halls burn it. Whole castle foundations are heated by it. Sealed **glasshouses** grow grain and greens through the winter, built into the stone below the keeps, safe from storms.
- Outside the South it is a luxury priced like spice. It grows nowhere else, the diggers can only bring up so much, and every basket must cross the Winterdoors pass or go the long way round. A fist-sized lump that burns three days in a great hall sells for a silver mark in Crownfold and for a copper in Frostholt.
- The Norvalen use it as **gift diplomacy**. A small iron brazier of blue flame is a present for a queen or a duke. It is also a quiet hint of the South's power.
- Rule for prose: the flame is blue, steady, silent and odorless. Do not say it "glows like" anything. Do not mention its source beyond "the mountains of the south."

## 3. The economic history

Before Frost-Glow was mined on a large scale, Southfrost bought **all** its fuel timber from Deepwood. The route was long, so Deepwood lords and the Crownfold factors who handled the trade charged ruinous prices. That bottleneck is why Southfrost was poor for centuries and why the Norvalen were seen as a minor house. The rise of Frost-Glow reversed it. Southfrost now buys almost no timber. Deepwood timber lords lost the southern market, which is one more reason the timber lords of the north-west (Fenlow of the western timber, the Selborn men at Pinefall) have a grudge against Frost-Glow.

*A useful link for the existing plot:* Fenlow's wealth is timber. Every winter that Frost-Glow becomes more common in the north is a winter his market shrinks. You do not need to say this in Book 1. It lets his motives deepen later with no changes to what is already written.

## 4. Southfrost's people and defenses

- Population just over half that of Crownfold or Rivermark. Small standing forces, spread thin.
- The **cold roads** are the mountain passes. They are patrolled all year against **reavers**, savage mountain raiders. The reavers live in the high valleys above the passes and come down in bands of twenty to a hundred.
- **The Winter Knights.** Perhaps six hundred knights in heavy white-lacquered plate that vanishes in a blizzard. They exist because the cold roads never stop needing them, and that is why Southfrost still has a knightly order after generations of peace in the interior. Under the old treaty system they are **loaned** to lords in the rest of the kingdom in wars, border troubles and internal quarrels. They return with money, rank, and stories. Between loans the home front is thinly held. The Loan is a standing tradition, not an exception.
- The Winter Knights are loyal to the Duke of Southfrost, not to the Brennan crown. The crown pays for them when it borrows them.
- The Duke of Southfrost is the head of House Norvalen. In the book's present, **Osric Norvalen** holds Frostholt. His father is dead (placeholder: old Duke **Ragnar Norvalen**, d. before Rhaenaya's marriage). Osric is addressed as Lord Osric until he is formally named Duke; the Twin War entries call him Duke Osric.

## 5. The Greyfang Isles

- A chain of bare, rocky islands out in the open ocean, south and west of Southfrost. (Placeholder name.)
- For centuries the islanders were disorganized raiders. They could have traded for Frost-Glow, and their pride made them steal it instead. They called buying it "begging from the mountain."
- The islands are ruled by a single crown for the first time: **King Haldar Skerrick, the Hundred-Skerry King** (placeholder). He is a brilliant, patient, ruthless warlord. He did not unite the islands by marrying or bargaining. He did it by burning the clans that refused and sparing those that knelt.
- His plan: take Southfrost, not raid it. Hold the mines and the cold roads, declare an independent fortress-kingdom, sell or withhold Frost-Glow to the north, and use the South as a base to reach the warm country. With Frost-Glow cut off, Crownfold nobles would turn to timber, timber prices would climb, and the northern great houses would bleed money in the winter.
- The islanders **look like Southerners** (see section 8). Long ago they were one people. The islanders say the Rathen road split them from their kin, and they use that grievance when they speak to the Southfrost smallfolk.

## 6. The traitor: Lord Ingvar Dask of Skarholt (placeholder)

- A lesser lord whose lands lie on the far western edge of Southfrost. Skarholt sits on the richest western mine, **the Weeping Vein**, and holds the forgotten mountain trails beside Deepwood, the only emergency route for timber.
- **The slight.** Old Duke Ragnar had promised Dask's daughter **Hild** to Osric as a bride, as the seal of a pact. In the same pact Dask was to be named **High Warden of the West**. Then Aldren VIII decided to marry Rhaenaya to Osric to secure the ice frontier, and the old Duke, who could not refuse his king, cancelled the pact. At the wedding feast Dask was seated below the salt and his daughter's betrothal gift was returned to him in front of the court. The wardenship went to a loyal central house, **House Vance of Whitlock** (placeholder).
- So the grievance is also against Foldhall: his daughter was traded away so a Brennan princess could have a husband. That ties him to Rhaenaya's marriage, a political marriage that has so far had only sweetness on the page.
- **The greed.** He already holds a third of the supply and all of the backup route. With the Islanders' backing he thinks he can charge the north any price. He does not dream of being richer than the king. He dreams of being richer than the Varens.
- **The deal.** Dask opens his ice-harbors and mountain trails to the Hundred-Skerry King. He believes he will be crowned King of the South, a puppet who still thinks he holds the strings.
- He should be sympathetic at first. His grievances are true. He is only wrong about what he should do with them.

## 7. The Frostglow War

- **Timing.** About fifteen to sixteen years into Rodrik's reign, when the twins and Alaric are sixteen. It comes after the Opening (see `ashen-wars-and-firedrakes.md`) and before the Second Ashen War.
- **Why Harwin.** Rodrik is tied down in the east against the firedrakes. He sends **Gareth** south with Thorn, and Harwin, the warrior twin, goes with him on his first real campaign, with Storm. Edric stays at Foldhall with his ink.
- **Who fights.** Gareth, Harwin, Alaric (riding Frost), the Winter Knights, and Osric. This is the war in which Alaric watches Harwin fight beside him, and why he later thinks Edric is "too soft."
- **How it ends.** The Islanders are broken at the Weeping Vein, not by Nyxens alone but by the Winter Knights holding the pass. Dask is taken alive. What Rodrik does with him is a choice for the book.
- The war is why the Southfrost lords want peace after it: they have seen how thin their defenses are.

## 8. How Southerners look

Describe, never explain. Northerners put it down to "the cold" or "the old blood." Nobody in the world knows the reasons.

- Tall and very broad, built like carters and smiths. Deep barrel chests.
- Rosy cheeks, even in summer.
- Long, narrow noses. Heavy, thick eyelids.
- Dense, coarse, oily hair that sheds snow and rain.
- Dark irises or very dark pupils.
- They barely sweat. They have **no smell**. Visitors comment on it ("you can't tell a Southerner is in the room until he speaks").
- The Greyfang Islanders share all of it.
- **Alaric** (half Southern): broad even as an infant, rosy-cheeked, with a thick coarse thatch of dark hair that carries a faint silver sheen from his mother. He is a big, solid, quiet boy. If you want a hook: Nyxens recognize their riders partly by scent, and Alaric is the child of a scentless people. His bond with Frost can be called an anomaly without anyone knowing why.
