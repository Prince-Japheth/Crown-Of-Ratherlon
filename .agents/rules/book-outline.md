## THE PITCH

A kingdom whose crown rests on a bloodline the Nyxens can smell goes to war with itself when twin princes, both legitimate, both riders, split the realm. The war ends with one survivor who cannot say which brother he was.

## HOW THE BOOK IS EXPERIENCED

The reader lives the story in the moment — Foldhall summers, campaigns, betrothal, alliances, the war — with NO opening frame.

Only at the END, after the cliff and the hunger to know who survived, does the book cut to old King Aldren X by the fire with his daughter Elowen. The cliffhanger is the recognition: the survivor is telling this wound to his child, and he still does not know which prince he was. Her next question seeds book two.

## BOOK ONE SPINE

0. Before the heirs (long): Rodrik and Elyn's Foldhall, family, visits, Nyxens, borders. Then pregnancy. Then birth and king's rush home. Then raising the twins, Roselyn, weather turns.
1. Succession crisis after King Aldren IX dies on campaign without naming an heir.
2. Two visions of rule: Edric (diplomatic) vs Harwin (warrior).
3. Lock-in: Harwin orders Lady Roselyn Varen killed.
4. First family war with Nyxens on both sides.
5. Final battle, one twin lives; identity unrecoverable.
6. End reveal / cliffhanger only: Aldren X + Elowen by the fire.

## WHAT THE STORY IS NOT

- Not a memoir announced on page one
- Not opening on pregnancy, cribs, or "who came first"
- Not "which twin was born first?" as the thesis of chapter one
- Not Nyxens as the main plot engine
- Not continent-conquest fanfic of Aegon

## WHY EDRIC = "DIPLOMATIC" / HARWIN = "WARRIOR"

Not labeled by narrator fiat — earned under Rodrik's reign through border, foreign, and domestic work.

### Edric (peace-craft, ink, quantity)
1. **Harbor peace:** settles Vine Shore / Western Shore dues quarrel (Varen vs Tarnell) without steel
2. **Mere ink:** mediates Rathen-cousin mill/hunt feud
3. **Truce words:** after First Ashen War, carries Foldhall's terms / hostage exchange to Eastverge
4. **Progress ear:** listens, names petitioners, buys loyalty with fairness

### Harwin (grit, quality, field)
1. **Eastverge seasons:** rides with Kelward — raid-answers, gullies, watch-stones, learned to sit a bront before most princes learn to sit a destrier
2. **First Ashen War:** at Rodrik's side in hard campaign while Edric's ink is elsewhere
3. **Icewide bruise:** cold-shore fight with Gareth / reavers
4. **Quality quarrel:** insists tallies without grit are corpses

Both true: Edric's peace is real; Harwin's grit is real. Camps form from *which fear* you trust after IX dies.

## WHY NO HEIR COMPETITION

1. Rodrik will not settle it while he lives — naming one splits the realm early. Then dies unexpectedly on campaign with no named heir.
2. A queen-mother cannot run a competence contest over adult princes. Both are of age, both riders, both with paramounts leaning. Elyn naming one = choosing a side.
3. The "first to sire an heir" rumor is already poison.
4. Readers who ask "why not a trial?" get this answer in lived politics, not a lecture.

## QUEEN ELYN'S FATE

**Suicide.** She kills herself when everything spirals:
1. Lost the love of her life (Rodrik / Aldren IX)
2. Not near done grieving him
3. Realm split in two because of the fruit of her womb
4. Boys she raised plunging into ruins the kingdom her Rathen ancestors put together

**Timing:** after Roselyn's murder and camps are irreversible, before/early in open Nyxen war. Not a prologue shock, not years after peace.

**Retired path:** Harwin kills her to stop an Edric announcement (creates two mother-crimes that stack into cartoon).

**Key irony:** She sees Edric in Rodrik's peace-craft and Harwin in Rodrik's grit. Rodrik knew which face each moment needed. She cannot crown that wholeness in either boy alone — that trap is part of why she breaks.

DO NOT have her announce a chosen heir. That contradicts the locked rule.

## WARRIOR TWIN CHARACTERIZATION

NO sympathy backstory. NO scarring childhood. He is genuinely cold by nature — good childhood, loving parents, loyal friends, every opportunity, and still becomes a terrible ruler.

He believes: strength is the highest virtue; compromise is weakness; mercy is weakness; fear is the most reliable foundation for peace. Those are philosophies, not traumas.

Arc: Not a good man becoming evil, but an already flawed man gaining enough power that his flaws become catastrophic.

The audience doesn't need to pity him. They only need to understand him.

## EDRIC'S MOTIVATIONS (Why he doesn't just surrender)

1. **Fiancée (Roselyn):** loves her genuinely; she talks about wanting to be queen — not scheming, just can't imagine a future without him on the throne
2. **Court pressure:** if he surrenders, warrior twin may kill him (he's still a contender), kill his children, punish his supporters
3. **Genuine belief:** he would be the better king; his brother's temperament will plunge realm into unnecessary wars
4. **Trapped by support:** every new ally makes peace harder; every victory makes compromise harder; he's no longer fighting for himself but for everyone who tied their fate to his

## END REVEAL (NOT a bookend open)

Do NOT put Aldren X and Elowen on the page until after the cliff battle.

Final chapter/epilogue: Years later. Southern snow. King Aldren X and Princess Elowen by the fire.
1. "Father... which prince were you?" — no answer.
2. "If I am your firstborn, and I am a daughter... what becomes of the Nyxens when I marry?" — cliffhanger into book two.

The survivor should NEVER learn the truth. Not in sequel, not through a secret diary. Never. The answer died on that cliff.

## OUTLINE STRUCTURE

### PART I: The Living Realm (~35-40%)
Make the kingdom sweet enough to hurt when it breaks. Raise the twins in view. Plant the fork without rushing the fall. Tone: adventure, domestic love, rising weather on borders.

**Movements:**
- I-0: Before the Heirs — Rodrik & Elyn's life with no children. Court, roosts, siblings, progress/border season, marriage as partnership. Pregnancy news at END of this movement.
- I-A: Arrival of the Twins — Pregnancy through birth. King rushes home from war to two cribs. Naming. Birth-order gossip starts as other people's noise.
- I-B: Boys of Foldhall — Childhood through early youth: yard, first litter, lessons, feast.
- I-C: Blood and Ink — Young men on campaign; Edric's ink, Harwin's mud; wings earned.
- I-D: Roselyn and the Realm at Ease — Courtship, betrothal, harvest progress.
- I-E: The Weather Turns — Borders wake; succession talk while father lives; last homecoming; he rides out to die.

### PART II: The Empty Chair (~15-20%)
Succession crisis earned. Camps form. Roselyn becomes the hinge. Tone: grief, letters, halls, last chance at coexistence.

Beats: King's death reaches Foldhall → Elyn trapped → Edric argues continuity, Harwin gathers hard country → Family picks sides (Cedric disobeys Rowan) → Swing politics → Roselyn's soft words → Roselyn's murder (Harwin's warrant).

### PART III: The Twin War (~25-30%)
Strategic civil war; first Nyxen-against-Nyxen; hollow victories. Tone: campaign seasons, doctrine, empty roosts.

Beats: Edric's grief becomes war doctrine → Rival legitimacy theater → First family rider death → Grain, winter, swing houses forced → Cedric/Elyra/Gareth arcs → Harwin fetches Oak → Both sides commit to decisive field.

### PART IV: The Cliff (~10-15%)
Destroy the question the war was fought to answer.

Beats: Failed almost-parley → Battle; twins fall together; one body comes back → Realm as Rorschach; crowning of Aldren X → Hard cut to end reveal.

## PLANNED SCENES & STORYTELLING DEVICES

### The "Bran's Lesson" Cinder Scene (MUST NOT BE FORGOTTEN)

A child-father dialogue that delivers the Brennan Cinder origin to the reader through natural character interaction — the way Bran learning from the Maester in AGOT teaches the reader houses, sigils, and history without a lecture.

Setup: A child asks their father why the Brennans look so beautiful, almost alien, if they descend from a simple farmer. The father tells the story of Aldren III the Adventurous:
- The restless prince, the most beautiful man in the realm, heir who wanted adventure
- His mother Queen Maris — the true beauty of the age, from whom he inherited his flawless looks
- The bargain: marry, produce an heir, then leave. Which he did.
- The voyage across the warm sea, gone for years, many believed dead
- The return: a massive shadow over the courtyard, a man who looked like a living god climbing down from his Nyxen. People swore it was an impostor.
- The Brennan Cinder: metallic ash-charcoal hair, a tribute to his beast's dark coat, made permanent by ancient sorcery
- The strangest part: the magic didn't die with him. It rewrote his bloodline, seized upon his existing beauty, and passed the enhanced beauty + Cinder hair to every Brennan who followed.

Tone: A father's bedtime story that happens to be true history. Warmth, wonder, a child's wide eyes. The reader absorbs deep lore the way the child absorbs a tale.

Placement: Somewhere in the book. Timing, setting, who the father and child are — all TBD, full creative liberty. Could be a bannerman's household, a holdfast, a village hearth. The characters don't need to be major cast.

### "Chronicles of the Realm" Device

In-world historical texts that can be quoted as epigraphs, read by characters, or referenced in dialogue — similar to the World of Ice and Fire histories. Passages about Nyxen lore, Brennan appearance, battle tactics, the dynasty's history, etc. Examples:
- "From the Chronicles of the Realm: On the Brennan Hounds of the Sky"
- Passages describing the Brennan beauty, the Nyxens in war, the metallic hair as a blood-token the beasts recognise

Use sparingly. Not every chapter. When a passage of living history would teach the reader something they need before the next scene delivers it.
