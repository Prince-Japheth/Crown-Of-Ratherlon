### The "Bran's Lesson" Cinder Scene (MUST NOT BE FORGOTTEN)

A child-father dialogue that delivers the Brennan Cinder origin to the reader through natural character interaction — the way Bran learning from the Maester in AGOT teaches the reader houses, sigils, and history without a lecture.

Setup: A child asks their father why the Brennans look so beautiful, almost alien, if they descend from a simple farmer. The father tells the story of Aldren III the Adventurous:
- The restless prince, the most beautiful man in the realm, heir who wanted adventure
- His mother Queen Maris — the true beauty of the age, from whom he inherited his flawless looks
- The bargain: marry, produce an heir, then leave. Which he did.
- The voyage across the warm sea, gone for years, many believed dead
- The return: a massive shadow over the courtyard, a man who looked like a living god climbing down from his Nyxen. People swore it was an impostor.
- The Brennan Cinder: metallic ash-charcoal hair, a tribute to his beast's dark coat, made permanent by ancient sorcery
- The strangest part: the magic didn't die with him. It rewrote his bloodline, seized upon his existing beauty, and passed the enhanced beauty + Cinder hair to every Brennan who followed.

Tone: A father's bedtime story that happens to be true history. Warmth, wonder, a child's wide eyes. The reader absorbs deep lore the way the child absorbs a tale.

Placement: Somewhere in the book. Timing, setting, who the father and child are — all TBD, full creative liberty. Could be a bannerman's household, a holdfast, a village hearth. The characters don't need to be major cast.

### "Chronicles of the Realm" Device

In-world historical texts that can be quoted as epigraphs, read by characters, or referenced in dialogue — similar to the World of Ice and Fire histories. Passages about Nyxen lore, Brennan appearance, battle tactics, the dynasty's history, etc. Examples:
- "From the Chronicles of the Realm: On the Brennan Hounds of the Sky"
- Passages describing the Brennan beauty, the Nyxens in war, the metallic hair as a blood-token the beasts recognise

Use sparingly. Not every chapter. When a passage of living history would teach the reader something they need before the next scene delivers it.
