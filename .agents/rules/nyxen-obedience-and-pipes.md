## Nyxen obedience and call-pipe use

- A Nyxen never refuses, ignores or drags its feet over a rider's command. No scene may show a Nyxen disobeying, turning of its own accord, following after being told to stay, or "deciding" against its rider. This includes veterans and includes kill-roars.
- Reluctance can be shown in the body: a flinch, a tremor, ears laid back, a low uneasy hum, a long look over the shoulder. The command is carried out at once anyway.
- Nyxens are docile, affectionate animals with preferences (fruit above all). A pup that has not yet bonded may wander, sniff and ignore people. A bonded adult does not.
- Every rider wears the call-pipe on a thin chain at the neck, like a necklace, and keeps it close. The pipe summons the rider's Nyxen from miles away, and the beast knows its own pipe's tone.
- A rider does not restrict a Nyxen's movements. No pens, gates, tethers, ropes or standing orders to stay home. The call-pipe reaches the Nyxen anywhere within miles, so there is never a reason to keep one close. Nyxens roam freely, and a bonded adult is never far from its rider.
- A rider tells a Nyxen to stay put only when something urgent or important is happening, and the Nyxen then holds still until released.
- Nyxens are very intelligent. In a tense situation (a fight in the yard, a frightened child, a bad day in the keep, a crowd with weapons) a Nyxen reads the room and holds back on its own, with no command at all. A Nyxen never takes off and roams into a tense scene.
- Another bloodline member can command a Nyxen that is not his own. It answers, a breath slower and with less ease than it answers its own rider.
- In battle a rider may give a Nyxen a **standing order** (for example, to keep harrying the enemy) before dismounting. The Nyxen carries it out until recalled by the rider's pipe. That is obedience, not choice.
