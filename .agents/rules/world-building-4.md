## GOVERNANCE

- **Hand of the King**: The seat is EMPTY at book open. Timeline: An old Lord Dorsen of Rivermark served as Hand under Aldren VIII. When Dorsen died, Aldren VIII appointed his eldest son **Rodrik** (Prince Rodrik, not yet king) as Hand. When Aldren VIII died and Rodrik became Aldren IX, he never selected a new Hand, as a king cannot be his own Hand. The seat has sat empty since his coronation, and Rowan is slowly filling the vacuum with his own ambition, doing the Hand's work without the title. Chapters 14 and 15 show Rowan hoping for the Hand while Rodrik intends the Master of Coin. Rowan is formally named Master of Coin in chapter 20, after the first-spring investigation. The Hand remains empty by deliberate royal policy: Rodrik keeps final decisions, Elyn holds petition court, and Rowan manages finance. Rowan’s later resentment is about being kept from the Hand and public authority, not remaining an untitled clerk for twenty years.
- **Small Council**: Exists in Foldhall, composed of the realm's highest officers who advise the King. The Small Council is currently fractured, lacking a Hand and arguing over permanent appointments, leading to paralysis that forces Rodrik to handle more than a king should.
- **Master of Coin**: The seat is currently contested. **Prince Rowan** acts in this capacity without the formal title while the Small Council bickers over a permanent appointment. He manages the ledgers, calculates the provisioning for marches, and fields the complaints over taxes and dues. (Backstory: he trained for seven years, from fourteen, under the previous Master of Coin, Lord Warin Lansor, who died a year before the coronation when his coach went off a washed-out ford. Aldren VIII never named a successor and told Rowan to keep the books. See aldren-viii-siblings-ages-and-offices.md.)
- **Master of Whisperers**: title exists — Aeion will question its wisdom as a character moment (see standing-directives.md). Currently held by **Lord Orston**.
- **Titles**: Duke (great house heads), Baron (lesser lords), Handler (Nyxen caretakers), Maester, Kingsguard
- **Address for the king**: "Your Grace" — NOT "My king"

### THE KINGSGUARD

Seven sworn swords who guard the king's person. Green cloaks (Brennan green) with a gold crown clasp at the throat. Led by the **Lord Commander of the Kingsguard.** Sworn for life; may not hold lands or titles while serving. Unlike ASOIAF's Kingsguard, they MAY marry — they are household knights, not monk-soldiers. When the king flies, at least two Kingsguard remain at the roost base; when on progress, all seven ride.

The Kingsguard guards the king, not the family. For the royal family's safety, the king assigns individual Kingsguard to attend specific members as needed.

### THE HEIR'S OWN

Three sworn swords assigned to the named heir. Green cloaks with a **silver** crown clasp (not gold — denotes service to the heir, not the king). Appointed by the king when an heir is formally named. When no heir is named, the Heir's Own stands empty — which is its own political statement. **At story open Rodrik has no children. After the twins’ birth, neither receives an Heir’s Own because Rodrik names neither heir.** The empty silver cloaks are a visible symbol of the succession crisis.

### BRENNAN HOUSE COLOURS — WHY GREEN AND GOLD

- **Gold**: Nyxens have tawny-gold fur, like a lion's coat. The Brennan dynasty's identity is inseparable from their Nyxens, so gold is the beast's colour on the banner. Also echoes the crown itself (gold metal).
- **Green**: The Brennans descend from Farmer Brennan, a man of the soil. Green for agriculture, for the fields of the Foldstead, for the roots the dynasty never forgot. A farmer's colour on a king's banner.

## RODRIK (ALDREN IX) AT STORY OPEN

- Crowned young (in his 20s); Aldren VIII died in peace, not late in life
- Has worn the crown only a season at chapter 1; Book 1 opens in Y0
- His Nyxen Hart: tawny-gold, veteran of the First Ashen War, still flinches at dry-road horns

- As prince: the First Ashen War, riding Hart beside Oak. As king: border-bands after Oak leaves, then the Opening and the fire years. His sons fight the Second Ashen War. Also one of the most diplomatic kings the realm ever had (brought the northern island into the kingdom without blood).
- **No heir at story open.** Rodrik and Elyn have no children yet. This constrains him — a king without an heir cannot risk himself on border skirmishes. Edwyn makes this argument when Kelward's raid news arrives — it's part of his play to volunteer himself and Elyra: "The king cannot go. You have no heir. If a gully swallows you, the crown falls to this room, and this room cannot agree on supper."

**Gareth's whereabouts at story open:** Not missing, not a mystery — just not at court by choice. Last known somewhere along the Southfrost or cold roads ("nearer the cold roads" — Elyra's words), riding with Norvalen patrols or hunting the reaver edge. Ravens reach him. The family knows roughly where he is. He took Thorn and went where the cold was. This matters for the Twin War: Gareth being close to Norvalen (Southfrost) seeds alliance geography early. Elyra's plan in Ch1 is to send word for Gareth to bend east and meet her at the Eastverge with Thorn (note: this Ch1 maneuvering is strictly to respond to a minor border skirmish during Rodrik's early reign, seeding their coordination well before the actual Twin War).

## FOLDHALL DETAILS

**Estate layout (locked):** The writer’s visual reference is Highgarden with a substantially larger compound. Foldhall is a spacious royal castle estate in the Crownfold river-country: extensive flower gardens, broad lawns, orchards, fields, open courtyards and connected halls and service buildings. The grounds provide room for Nyxens to roam, graze and rest. The city lies below the castle grounds. Eastern cliffs accommodate the open roost bays; they do not make Foldhall a city clinging to a mountainside. Preserve the estate’s beauty, abundance and scale in views of the royal seat.

Slate stone ramparts, eastern cliff-barns (roost yards for Nyxens), a solar, a yard, a lesser hall, a gate, an oath-yard. Wakes "ovens first, then the yard, then the roosts." Petition ribbons (color-coded). Lesser hall used for minor court business.

## NAMING RULES

**Register:** Names should feel like the Andals and First Men of Westeros — native medieval stock, NOT Valyrian. Short-medium names, hard consonants, Anglo-Norman / Germanic feel.

**Avoid:** Valyrian perfume (*ae* tics, *-ys/-ysha*), engineered fantasy endings (*-xen, -vex, -axis*), Mediterranean romance-novel princely names.

**Places — avoid:** *march/marches/Marcher*, *-hill*, more *-hall* seats (Foldhall is the only one), lazy *heartland/peace-lands/Summer Reach*, house-name + geography sticker seats (*Rathenmere* etc.).

**How royal names work:** Every crowned king takes **Aldren** and abandons his birth name in public. Aldren IX = Rodrik (same man, two names). Never invent a third name.

## THE CALL-PIPE (Expanded Lore)

### Inventor & Origin

**Aldren III (the Adventurous) brought the call-pipe concept from Aurouvah.** During his years abroad, Leofric saw Aurouvian beast-keepers summon specific creatures from miles away using tuned mouth instruments whose tones encoded identity. He brought the idea home alongside Saeraphyn and the Cinder. Upon his restoration as king, he commissioned the first call-pipes for the Brennan riders.

**The Maester of Foldhall engineered the craft.** Maesters are an ancient institution in Ratherlon, predating any Brennan king (like the Citadel predates Aegon). What Aldren III imported was the concept; the Maester solved the acoustics — boring the interior, cutting protruding notches whose count shapes the harmonic. Only the Maester of Foldhall holds the bore geometry and notching technique. The knowledge passes by oral apprenticeship, Maester to Maester. No written blueprint exists.

### The Rider Roll

The roll begins with **Aldren III as Ordinal 1**. Riders before him — Aldren I, Aldren II, and their kin — are **"riders of the old way,"** uncounted and un-piped. They called their Nyxens by voice, by habit, by the bond itself. The numbered pipe is a royal institution, not a natural law.

**Roll-keeper:** The Maester of Foldhall. The rider roll is a physical document — vellum, the Maester's hand — kept alongside the pipe collection in the Foldhall armoury.

**Tristan’s admission (locked):** Edwyn’s firstborn, bastard son Tristan is a proven anomaly rider and Rodrik’s immediate nephew. The controversy concerns recognizing him on this roll and awarding his ordinal and call-pipe despite his illegitimacy. Edwyn wins that recognition on the grounds that Tristan is his son and a rider already accepted by Ysehnys. Entry records the bond; it does not create it, legitimize his birth, or award inheritance rights. His exact ordinal is not yet assigned in the author’s map.

### The Fairest Pipes

Certain notch-counts produce a tone so pure that even the human ear hears the difference. This is acoustics, not magic: certain counts align the bore harmonics into something unmistakably beautiful, the way certain string lengths produce consonant intervals. Multiple counts across the range are "fair," but they are rare — most pipes sound alike to everyone but the Nyxen.

**The most celebrated sweet pipe** in Foldhall's memory belongs to an otherwise unremarkable rider — not a king, not a war hero, not a firstborn. The pipe outlived the rider's name in common memory; people remember the tone, not the person. The most beautiful sound in the dynasty belongs to someone history barely remembers.

### Remake, Theft & Death Rules

- **The Heir's Cage (War Tradition):** The Brennans have an unspoken tradition: because the crown relies on air superiority, a reigning king will bring his younger riding children with him to war for air support. However, he will **strictly forbid the heir** from riding to battle, terrified of losing the fragile firstborn Nyxen-scent lineage. This creates a deeply toxic family dynamic: the heir feels restless, emasculated, and jealous of being locked in Foldhall's halls while their siblings earn glory. Meanwhile, the younger siblings feel a bitter envy, realizing their father treats them as disposable weapons of war who can die in the mud, while guarding his "precious" heir at all costs. (This perfectly explains why Rodrik, the moment he becomes king, rushes out to fight his own wars—he spent his youth locked up while his father used younger riding kin on later border duties. It also explains why Rodrik breaks this exact tradition by refusing to stop Aeian from fighting, not wanting to inflict the same cage on his son.)

- **Rider dies:** The pipe is kept in the Foldhall armoury as a relic. Never destroyed, never reassigned. The ordinal is retired permanently.
- **Pipe lost:** A replacement is made by the Maester of Foldhall — same ordinal, same notch-count, same tone. But this barely ever happens; pipes are kept close and rarely leave a rider's person.
- **Pipe stolen:** Exceedingly rare. A Nyxen knows where its rider is and can distinguish its rider's breath and position from a stranger sounding the same pipe miles away. A thief with a stolen pipe summons nothing but attention.
