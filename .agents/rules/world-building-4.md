## GOVERNANCE

- **Hand of the King**: The seat is EMPTY at book open. Timeline: An old Lord Dorsen of Rivermark served as Hand under Aldren VIII. When Dorsen died, Aldren VIII appointed his eldest son **Rodrik** (Prince Rodrik, not yet king) as Hand. When Aldren VIII died and Rodrik became Aldren IX, he never selected a new Hand — a king cannot be his own Hand. The seat has sat empty since his coronation, and Rowan is slowly filling the vacuum with his own ambition, doing the Hand's work without the title.
- **Master of Whisperers**: title exists — Edric will question its wisdom as a character moment (see standing-directives.md)
- **Titles**: Duke (great house heads), Baron (lesser lords), Handler (Nyxen caretakers), Maester, Kingsguard
- **Address for the king**: "Your Grace" — NOT "My king"

### THE KINGSGUARD

Seven sworn swords who guard the king's person. Green cloaks (Brennan green) with a gold crown clasp at the throat. Led by the **Lord Commander of the Kingsguard.** Sworn for life; may not hold lands or titles while serving. Unlike ASOIAF's Kingsguard, they MAY marry — they are household knights, not monk-soldiers. When the king flies, at least two Kingsguard remain at the roost base; when on progress, all seven ride.

The Kingsguard guards the king, not the family. For the royal family's safety, the king assigns individual Kingsguard to attend specific members as needed.

### THE HEIR'S OWN

Three sworn swords assigned to the named heir. Green cloaks with a **silver** crown clasp (not gold — denotes service to the heir, not the king). Appointed by the king when an heir is formally named. When no heir is named, the Heir's Own stands empty — which is its own political statement. **At story open, neither twin has been given an Heir's Own, because Rodrik never named an heir.** The empty silver cloaks are a visible symbol of the succession crisis.

### BRENNAN HOUSE COLOURS — WHY GREEN AND GOLD

- **Gold**: Nyxens have tawny-gold fur, like a lion's coat. The Brennan dynasty's identity is inseparable from their Nyxens, so gold is the beast's colour on the banner. Also echoes the crown itself (gold metal).
- **Green**: The Brennans descend from Farmer Brennan, a man of the soil. Green for agriculture, for the fields of the Foldstead, for the roots the dynasty never forgot. A farmer's colour on a king's banner.

**Lord naming convention in prose:**
- *Lord Roland Varen of Rosegate*
- *Lord Osric Norvalen of Frostholt*
- *Lord Simon Kelward of Eastgirt*
- *Lady (or Lord) of Stillford* for the Rathen paramount
- King: *King Aldren IX of House Brennan, of Foldhall*

- Crowned young (in his 20s); Aldren VIII died in peace, not late in life
- Has worn the crown only a few years (~2-4)

- Private affectionate relationship with Hart — grooms him with nyxen oil, talks nonsense to him
- Hart's handler: Oren — "a Foldhall man named Oren with mash stains older than Rodrik's crown"
- As prince: miniature border adventures — broke tradition by going to war before having children, severally against his father Aldren VIII's commands. As king: First Ashen War then uneasy quiet; wars thicken later. Also one of the most diplomatic kings the realm ever had (brought the NW island into the kingdom without blood).
- **No heir at story open.** Rodrik and Elyn have no children yet. This constrains him — a king without an heir cannot risk himself on border skirmishes. Edwyn makes this argument when Kelward's raid news arrives — it's part of his play to volunteer himself and Elyra: "The king cannot go. You have no heir. If a gully swallows you, the crown falls to this room, and this room cannot agree on supper."

**Gareth's whereabouts at story open:** Not missing, not a mystery — just not at court by choice. Last known somewhere along the Southfrost or cold roads ("nearer the cold roads" — Elyra's words), riding with Norvalen patrols or hunting the reaver edge. Ravens reach him. The family knows roughly where he is. He took Thorn and went where the cold was. This matters for the Twin War: Gareth being close to Norvalen (Southfrost) seeds alliance geography early. Elyra's plan in Ch1 is to send word for Gareth to bend east and meet her at the Eastverge with Thorn (note: this Ch1 maneuvering is strictly to respond to a minor border skirmish during Rodrik's early reign, seeding their coordination well before the actual Twin War).

**Geographic confirmation:** Crownfold (Foldhall) is dead center of the kingdom. An overnight Nyxen flight from the Eastverge (eastern border, bordering the Ashlands) to Foldhall is plausible — roughly half the kingdom's width, hours by air vs days on horseback.

## NAMING RULES

**Register:** Names should feel like the Andals and First Men of Westeros — native medieval stock, NOT Valyrian. Short-medium names, hard consonants, Anglo-Norman / Germanic feel.

**Avoid:** Valyrian perfume (*ae* tics, *-ys/-ysha*), engineered fantasy endings (*-xen, -vex, -axis*), Mediterranean romance-novel princely names.

**Places — avoid:** *march/marches/Marcher*, *-hill*, more *-hall* seats (Foldhall is the only one), lazy *heartland/peace-lands/Summer Reach*, house-name + geography sticker seats (*Rathenmere* etc.).

**How royal names work:** Every crowned king takes **Aldren** and abandons his birth name in public. Aldren IX = Rodrik (same man, two names). Never invent a third name.

## THE CALL-PIPE (Expanded Lore)

### Inventor & Origin

**Aldren III (the Adventurous) brought the call-pipe concept from Aurouvah.** During his years abroad, Leofric saw Aurouvian beast-keepers summon specific creatures from miles away using tuned mouth instruments whose tones encoded identity. He brought the idea home alongside Seraphine and the Cinder. Upon his restoration as king, he commissioned the first call-pipes for the Brennan riders.

**The Maester of Foldhall engineered the craft.** Maesters are an ancient institution in Ratherlon, predating any Brennan king (like the Citadel predates Aegon). What Aldren III imported was the concept; the Maester solved the acoustics — boring the interior, cutting protruding notches whose count shapes the harmonic. Only the Maester of Foldhall holds the bore geometry and notching technique. The knowledge passes by oral apprenticeship, Maester to Maester. No written blueprint exists.

### The Rider Roll

The roll begins with **Aldren III as Ordinal 1**. Riders before him — Aldren I, Aldren II, and their kin — are **"riders of the old way,"** uncounted and un-piped. They called their Nyxens by voice, by habit, by the bond itself. The numbered pipe is a royal institution, not a natural law.

**Roll-keeper:** The Maester of Foldhall. The rider roll is a physical document — vellum, the Maester's hand — kept alongside the pipe collection in the Foldhall armoury.

### The Fairest Pipes

Certain notch-counts produce a tone so pure that even the human ear hears the difference. This is acoustics, not magic: certain counts align the bore harmonics into something unmistakably beautiful, the way certain string lengths produce consonant intervals. Multiple counts across the range are "fair," but they are rare — most pipes sound alike to everyone but the Nyxen.

**The most celebrated sweet pipe** in Foldhall's memory belongs to an otherwise unremarkable rider — not a king, not a war hero, not a firstborn. The pipe outlived the rider's name in common memory; people remember the tone, not the person. The most beautiful sound in the dynasty belongs to someone history barely remembers.

### Remake, Theft & Death Rules

- **Rider dies:** The pipe is kept in the Foldhall armoury as a relic. Never destroyed, never reassigned. The ordinal is retired permanently.
- **Pipe lost:** A replacement is made by the Maester of Foldhall — same ordinal, same notch-count, same tone. But this barely ever happens; pipes are kept close and rarely leave a rider's person.
- **Pipe stolen:** Exceedingly rare. A Nyxen knows where its rider is and can distinguish its rider's breath and position from a stranger sounding the same pipe miles away. A thief with a stolen pipe summons nothing but attention.
