APPENDIX
A Dictionary of the Realm
Words you may meet in the telling.
◆
49
A

Aldren. The regnal name taken by every crowned king of House Brennan in the Kingdom of Ratherlon. A man may be born Rodrik, or another name; once crowned, the realm calls him Aldren, with a number.

appanage. Land, rents, or a seat granted by the king to a younger child or close kin so they can keep their own household, without splitting the whole realm. The grantee still owes the crown.

B

banner. The cloth device of a house, flown on poles and over gates. To raise a banner is to declare for a lord or cause.

bannerman. A lesser noble sworn to a duke; he owes service and fights under that duke’s banner.

baron. A lesser noble holding land under a duke; he owes loyalty and service to the great house above him.

bront. A massive single-horned beast of the Eastverge, taller than any horse, with a dense hide and a single forward-sweeping horn of bone. Named for the sound of their charge. Kelward cavalry ride bronts instead of horses; a charging line of them is a living shield wall. Wild herds graze beyond the watch-stones. Stubborn, ill-tempered, tireless, fearless.

birth name. The name a royal person carries before crowning. King Aldren IX’s birth name is Rodrik.

C

Crownfold, the. The countryside and river-country around Foldhall. Foldhall is the seat; the Crownfold is the land that answers it first.

crown's watchman. A man sworn to hold the outer watch under the king's authority, not a lord's household guard. To kill a crown's watchman is to strike at the throne itself.

D

Deepwood, the. Southern forest country under the mountain shadow; seat of House Selborn at Pinefall.

demesne. Land held directly by a duke or the king, not merely watched over through lesser vassals.
duke. The head of a great house; highest rank beneath the king. A duke rules his seat and the wide country around it.

E

Eastgirt. Seat of House Kelward; dry watch country of the Eastverge.

Eastverge, the. Dry eastern frontier of towers and gullies. Beyond it lies the Ashen Kingdom.

F

Follhall. The royal seat of House Brennan: castle, household, and the city tight about them.

smallfolk. The common people of Ratherlon: farmers, tradesmen, fishermen, innkeepers — anyone without noble blood or title. Also called commonfolk.

frugivore. An animal that lives chiefly on fruit. Nyxens are frugivores and browse-eaters, not meat-hunters by nature.

G

gate-warden. The officer who keeps a castle gate: who enters, who leaves, who is announced.

good-brother. A brother-in-law. Queen Elyn’s good-brother is her husband’s brother. Likewise: good-sister, good-father, good-mother. These are kinship words, not praise.

great house. One of the paramount houses of the realm. Its head holds the rank of duke and rules a wide region under the king.

H

holdfast. A strong dwelling or small fortified place; broader than castle.

house. A noble family and all who swear to its name. Spoken as House Brennan, House Varen, and so on.

house words. A house’s motto or saying. For House Brennan: By Wing We Hold.

I

Icewide, the. The cold sea to the south of the realm; feared by sailors in hard years.
K

kin. Blood relations; one’s family by birth or marriage.

Kingsguard, the. Seven sworn swords who guard the king’s person. They wear green cloaks with a gold crown clasp at the throat and serve for life. Led by the Lord Commander of the Kingsguard.

Heir’s Own, the. Three sworn swords assigned to the named heir, wearing green cloaks with a silver crown clasp. Appointed by the king when an heir is formally named. When the Heir’s Own stands empty, no heir has been declared.

L

ledger. A bound book of accounts: money owed, money paid, stores counted, harbour dues.

litter. A group of Nyxen young born together. Children of the blood are often shown a litter of pups to choose from.

lord, lady. Forms of address for ranked nobility. A duke or baron may be called my lord in courtesy; his wife my lady. A woman holding rank in her own right is a lady of her seat.

M

Merelands, the. Lake and mill country; seat of House Rathen at Stillford. Rathen were kings of Ratherlon before Brennan.

N

Nyxen. A great flying beast of the realm. Docile by nature; eats fruit and browse. Recognizes a rare scent in the old farmer’s bloodline. Raised from a pup, named by its raiser, trained until it can be ridden. Keen of ear and nose. Flight uses learned weight-lightening. A rider summons their own with a call-pipe. Most die of grief if their human dies.

Nyxen oil. Oil used in the roosts for hide, tackle, and care. Its smell clings to Foldhall’s stones.

P

paramount. Highest under the king in a region; a great house that other local houses swear to.
pipe, call-pipe. A small mouth instrument made for a Nyxen rider when they and a beast choose each other. The rider’s number on the rider roll is cut as protruding notches inside the pipe; those notches shape the tone. A Nyxen knows its own across miles.

progress. A royal journey through the realm, visiting seats and countries.

pup. A young Nyxen, still growing; playful, not yet large enough to ride.

R

Ratherlon. The kingdom’s formal name. Everyday speech often says only the realm. The land’s name is older than House Brennan; it stayed when the dynasty changed.

realm, the. Everyday speech for the Kingdom of Ratherlon.

reeve. A village officer appointed to manage the day-to-day running of a manor’s farmland. He tracks planting, debts, and small disputes on behalf of the lord. Not noble — a trusted commoner with local authority.

regnal name. The name used while reigning. For Brennan kings of Ratherlon, always Aldren.

rider. One who has raised or been accepted by a Nyxen and flies with it. Riders are few.

rider roll. The running count of Brennan Nyxen riders since Aldren I.

roar. The Nyxen’s directed sound-blast: a pressure-shout from chest and throat that can stagger or kill at close range. Not flame.

roost. Where Nyxens are kept and housed. Also used for the place a beast settles to rest.

roost-cold. The chill left on a bed when someone rose early and went out to the roosts, not the deeper empty cold of a spouse away at war. Household slang.

S

seat. A house’s chief home, the castle or hall from which it rules.

sept-yard. The open yard or walk beside Foldhall’s place of worship. People cut across it on errands; it is not only for prayer.
shout upwind. To call so the wind carries your voice toward someone. A half-joke for go find him where the beasts are.

shutters. Wooden panels that close over a window. Open for air and light; shut against cold, storm, or night.

sigil. A house’s badge on banners, shields, and seals. House Brennan’s sigil is a Nyxen on green.

solar. A private upper chamber in a castle, often well-lit, used by the lord or lady for quiet work and family, not the great hall.

Southfrost, the. Cold southern mountain country; seat of House Norvalen at Frostholt. Winter comes from this direction.

steward. The officer who runs a household’s stores, staff, and daily order.

Stillford. Seat of House Rathen in the Merelands. Named for the still-waters ford, not for the house.

V

vassal. A house or noble sworn to a greater one; a baron is vassal to a duke, and a duke to the king.

Vine Shore, the. Warm northern coast of wine, citrus, and trade; seat of House Varen at Rosegate.

W

wall-walk. The path along the top of a castle wall, where watchers stand.

warden. A keeper or guardian of a place or duty.

Warm Sea, the. The warm northern water off the Vine Shore. Trade crowns lie beyond it.

Western Shore, the. The realm’s western sea-coast; seat of House Tarnell at Saltcrown.

words. See house words.

Y

yard. The open court inside castle walls: cobbles, stables, noise, arrivals, and leave-takings.
