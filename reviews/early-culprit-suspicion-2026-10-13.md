The hired attackers and their intermediary remain established. A local accent does not establish the patron. The existing Fenlow/Pell evidence and later coin-trace remain intact. Roland’s grain coercion and resistance to scrutiny are real conduct with consequences, not fabricated evidence that disappears when the murder inquiry turns elsewhere.

## Clarification: beliefs are permitted; author endorsement is not

Characters may sincerely defend or accuse Roland, including declaring that he would never order such a killing. Do not treat that opinion as the narrative’s settled answer or flag the evidence as a decoy. Readers form their judgments from competing perspectives and actual events.

Chapter 1 now retains Rodrik’s personal belief: `“I do not believe Roland ordered this,” Rodrik said.` Rowan answers: `“You know Roland,” Rowan said. “Do you know every man who wears his colours?”` Rodrik then asks whether anything was taken. This replaces the previous bare transition to that question; investigation continues without author confirmation of either position.
